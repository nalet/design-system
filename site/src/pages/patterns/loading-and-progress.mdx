# loading and progress

what to show while something loads: nothing for a blink, the value when the progress is known, the loader when it isn't, and the shape of the content for a list or a page.

## which one

| situation | use |
| --- | --- |
| under about 0.4 s | nothing: a loader that flashes reads as a glitch |
| known progress (a copy, an upload) | a progress bar with the value: `62% · 1.4 of 2.2 gb` |
| unknown, inline (a button, a field, a toolbar) | the loader at 16px; the label stays |
| unknown, a region or a player | the loader at 32–48px, with one line of text |
| a list or a page | skeleton blocks in the shape of the content, not a loader in empty space |
| reduced motion | the loader stands still, and the text carries the state |
| assistive technology | one status with a label, announced once |

## the loader

a faint, still square frame, with three quarters of it lit and the gap travelling round at a steady pace, one lap in four seconds; still under reduced motion. the frames of the system are square, and only the moving gap says "working".

<Mock planned="the square loader" label="the loader's shape, still">
  <LoaderSizes />
</Mock>

- **inline**, at 16px: in a button, a field or a toolbar, before the label, which stays.
- **in a region** or a player, at 32–48px, centred, with one line of text under it that says what is loading.
- **with reduced motion** it stands still — the frame and its three lit quarters — and the text says that something is loading.

today: the <C slug="spinner" /> is a turning ring: a faint circle with a lit quarter that turns, 0.7s a turn. under reduced motion it turns more slowly, once every 1.6s, rather than standing still. the square loader is **planned**.

## known progress

when the work can say how far it has come — a copy, an upload, a scan of a library — show a bar and the value in words: the percentage and the amount, `62% · 1.4 of 2.2 gb`. the words are what people read; the bar is how far, at a glance.

today: there is no progress bar component. it is **planned**.

## unknown, in a region

a panel, a player or a detail that loads on its own shows the loader at 32–48px with one line of text: what is loading, in the object's words.

## a list or a page

a list or a page that loads for the first time shows blocks in the shape of what is coming: rows of the length its rows will have, a title where its title will be. the page doesn't jump when the content arrives, and people can see what kind of thing is on its way.

## behaviour

- **wait before showing anything.** a load that finishes within about 0.4 s shows nothing; the loader appears only after that, so a fast answer doesn't flash.
- **show it where the content is.** a busy button keeps its place and its label; a busy list keeps its frame and its rows.
- **one loader per region.** a page that loads three panels shows three loaders, each where its panel is — not one over the whole page.
- **stop the moment it is done**, and replace the loader with the content, or with what went wrong — see [empty states](/design-system/patterns/empty-states/) and [message handling](/design-system/patterns/message-handling/).

today: the components show their busy state the moment it is set — a button's `loading`, a data table's `loading`. waiting 0.4 s first is the app's part.

## accessibility

- **one status, with a label, announced once**: "loading the episode list". not a stream of updates, and not every few percent of a progress bar.
- the area that is loading says so too: a busy list or button is marked busy for assistive tech.
- motion never says it alone: with reduced motion the loader stands still, and the text carries the state.

today: the spinner is a status named "loading"; a busy button and a busy data table mark themselves busy.

## content

- **the line of text** — what is loading, in the object's words: `loading the episode list`, `asking the catalog`. no "please wait", no "almost there".
- **the value** — the percentage, then the amount: `62% · 1.4 of 2.2 gb`, with thousands separators and the unit.
- **when it takes long** — say what is happening, not that it is slow: `scanning 2,400 of 9,000 files`.
