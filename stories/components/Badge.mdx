# badge

a small square label: mono, lowercase, hairline frame, 20px tall. badge is for labels, counts and tags. the state of an object — running, failed, needs attention — is [object status](?path=/docs/components-feedback-status-object-status--docs), not a badge.

<Canvas of={BadgeStories.Playground} />

## when to use

- to tag an item with a short attribute that helps people scan and tell items apart: `gpu`, `read-only`, `4k`, `hdr`.
- for a count beside the thing it counts: `12 episodes`, `3 new`.
- for a short label that stays true for as long as the item exists: a version (`v2.4.1`), a `default` marker.

## when not to use

- **for the state of an object.** use [object status](?path=/docs/components-feedback-status-object-status--docs). it maps each state to a semantic colour and its own glyph, and announces warnings and errors in words. a badge's tone is only a colour: nothing stops a badge from saying `failed` in green.
- **for a message about the page or a task** — saved, could not load. use a [message strip](?path=/docs/components-feedback-status-message-strip--docs), or a [toast](?path=/docs/components-feedback-status-toast--docs) for something that just finished.
- **as a button or a filter chip.** a badge is a `span`: it can't take focus or be pressed. use a [button](?path=/docs/components-actions-button--docs), or [filter tabs](?path=/docs/components-navigation-filter-tabs--docs) to filter a list.
- **for a count on a launchpad tile.** the [tile](?path=/docs/components-navigation-tile--docs) has its own `badge`.

## anatomy

1. **container** — inline, 20px tall, padding 0 8px, 1px border, square. `neutral` sits on `--surface-2` with a `--border-2` frame; a tone sits on a 12% tint of its colour over `--bg-2`, framed in a 45% mix of its colour and `--border-2`.
2. **dot** (optional) — a 6px square in the label's colour, 8px before the text. hidden from assistive tech.
3. **label** — jetbrains mono 11px, letter-spacing 0.02em, rendered lowercase, never wraps.

## variants and states

four tones. `neutral` is the default and the right choice for most tags; a tone is only a colour, so use one where it helps people find a label, and keep its meaning the same everywhere.

| tone | text | use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `neutral` | `--fg-muted` | tags, counts, versions: the default |
| `blue` | `--cloud-blue` | information worth spotting: `new`, `default` |
| `green` | `--signal-green` | a positive attribute: `verified`, `included` |
| `amber` | `--signal-amber` | an attribute that asks for care: `read-only`, `deprecated` |

there is no red tone. an error is a state: [object status](?path=/docs/components-feedback-status-object-status--docs) for one object, a [message strip](?path=/docs/components-feedback-status-message-strip--docs) for the page.

## behaviour and keyboard

a badge is static: a `span` with no focus and no keys of its own.

| key | does |
| --- | --- |
| `tab` | skips it. a badge is not focusable and has no action. |

- it never wraps (`white-space: nowrap`) and has no maximum width: a long label runs past its container rather than breaking.

## accessibility

- a plain `span` with no role. screen readers read its text inline with what's around it.
- the tone is never announced, and the dot is `aria-hidden`. the words carry the meaning: `read-only` is read, the amber is not.
- css lowercases the label; screen readers read the text as you wrote it.
- a bare number is read without its subject ("12"). keep a count right next to what it counts, or say it: `12 files`.
- don't attach `onClick`: the `span` can't be focused or pressed from the keyboard. an action is a [button](?path=/docs/components-actions-button--docs).
- every tone's text meets 4.5:1 on its own tint; `green` is the closest, at about 4.9:1. the frame is decorative.

## content

- one or two words, lowercase, no full stop: `gpu`, `read-only`, `3 new`.
- describe what the item is, not what it is doing right now. "running" is a state; "gpu" is a tag.
- digits for counts: `12`, not `twelve`.
- don't repeat the column or field name in every badge: `gpu`, not `label: gpu`.

## do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="badges as tags: what the item is. neutral unless a colour helps.">
    <Badge>gpu</Badge>
    <Badge>batch</Badge>
    <Badge>stateful</Badge>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="a state in a badge. the tone is only a colour, so nothing stops it saying failed in green. use object status.">
    <Badge tone="green">failed</Badge>
    <Badge tone="amber">running</Badge>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="neutral by default, one tone where it helps people find a label.">
    <Badge>v2.4.1</Badge>
    <Badge>12 files</Badge>
    <Badge tone="blue">new</Badge>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="a colour on every tag means nothing; people look for a pattern that isn't there.">
    <Badge tone="blue">gpu</Badge>
    <Badge tone="green">batch</Badge>
    <Badge tone="amber">http</Badge>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="a word or two. the explanation goes in text beside it.">
    <Badge tone="amber">read-only</Badge>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="a sentence in a badge never wraps; it runs out of its container.">
    <Badge tone="amber">read-only until the nightly backup has finished</Badge>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

## props

`Badge` passes every native `<span>` attribute through (`id`, `title`, `aria-*`, …) and forwards its ref to the `span`.
