# shell bar

The <Term id="shell-bar">shell bar</Term> runs across the top of every screen: on the <Term id="portal">portal</Term>'s start page, in every <Term id="app">app</Term> inside it, and in every product opened from it. It always answers the same three questions: where am I, is there anything new for me, and where is my account?

Because it looks and works the same everywhere, people learn it once, and always know where to look.

<figure class="ds-figure">

The shell bar, its user menu and the avatar are planned components; the drawings on this page show the idea.

## what it holds

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <ShellBarAnatomy />
  <figcaption>The shell bar, close up, from left to right.</figcaption>
</figure>

1. **The product's name**, led by the blue chevron. It takes people home: to the [launchpad](/design-system/patterns/launchpad-and-tiles/), or to the product's start page.
2. **The app's name**, next to it, when an app is open: “/ downloads”. People always know where they are.
3. **Search**, to find apps and things from anywhere. This is planned.
4. **Notifications**: a bell with the number of unread notices. It opens one panel with the notices of every app.
5. **The <Term id="user-menu">user menu</Term>**, behind a small square with the person's picture or initials, their <Term id="avatar">avatar</Term>. It opens one menu for everything about the person.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <UserMenuOpen />
  <figcaption>The user menu: who is signed in, then account, settings and help, and sign out last.</figcaption>
</figure>

The menu starts with the person's name and email address, so they can see who is signed in. Then come account, settings (only for people who may change them), and help. Sign out comes last, set apart, so nobody presses it by accident.

## the same everywhere

- **One user menu, in every product.** No email address shown in the bar, and no sign-out button of its own.
- **The avatar is square**, like every frame in the system. A photo is cropped to a square.
- **A product for consumers can keep its own main navigation in the bar**, such as home, films and series. The product's name, the notifications and the user menu stay where the <Term id="shell">shell</Term> puts them, and look the way the shell draws them.
- **The product's name holds still.** The bar is always in view, so its chevron doesn't blink; see [brand](/design-system/styles/brand/#accessibility).

## what doesn't go in it

The bar belongs to the shell, so it only holds what every screen shares.

- **An app's own actions** belong in the app's header or its <C slug="toolbar">toolbar</C>.
- **Moving between an app's views** is the app's own [navigation](/design-system/patterns/app-navigation/).
- **An app's settings** are reached through the user menu: its settings list each app's setup and link straight to it. See [settings and setup](/design-system/patterns/settings-and-setup/).

## accessibility

Proposed, together with the components:

- The bar is the page's banner, which <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> users can jump to. The product's name is a link named after where it goes, such as the product's name and “home”.
- The bell is a button whose name includes the count: “notifications, 2 unread”.
- The avatar is a button whose name says what it shows and what it opens: “au, user menu”. Inside the menu, the arrow keys move between the items, and Esc closes it and puts the <Term id="focus">focus</Term> back on the avatar, as in the <C slug="action-menu" />.

## the words

- **The product's name.** The product's own name, lowercase, as on its tile.
- **The app's name.** The same word as the app's tile on the launchpad.
- **The menu's header.** The person's name, then their email address, quieter.
- **The menu's items.** Account, settings, help and sign out, in that order.
