# action menu

An action menu keeps several actions behind one small button, so a table row or a toolbar stays calm until someone needs one of them. The menu opens above the page, so nothing around it can cut it off.

<Example label="an action menu on a row, live: open it and pick an action">
  <InARow client:visible />
</Example>

## when to use

- For the actions on one thing, such as one row of a table: “pick a release”, “grab now”. One menu per row keeps a table calm.
- As the <Term id="overflow-menu">overflow menu</Term> of a toolbar, for the actions that don't fit. The <C slug="toolbar">toolbar</C> does this on its own, behind “more actions”.
- For a few related commands behind a button with words on it: “export”, then “as csv” or “as json”.

## when to use something else

- **For the one <Term id="emphasized-action">emphasized action</Term> of a view.** Show it as a <C slug="button" />. Hidden in a menu, nothing reads as the next step.
- **For actions on several selected rows.** Use a <C slug="toolbar">selection toolbar</C>, which says how many rows it acts on.
- **To choose a value, such as a sort order or a filter.** A menu runs commands, and it doesn't show the current choice. Use a <C slug="select" />, or a <C slug="multi-select" /> for several values. One value from a few, behind an icon in a toolbar or a player's controls, such as a video's quality, is the planned [menu of one choice](#a-menu-of-one-choice).
- **To go to another page.** Use a link. A menu item is a button, so it can't be opened in a new tab.
- **To ask for a decision.** Use a <C slug="modal" />.

## anatomy

1. **<Term id="trigger">Trigger</Term>.** The button that opens the menu. Usually it is a small, quiet <C slug="icon-button" /> with three little squares in a row. With words on it instead, it is a standard <C slug="button" /> with your text and a small downward arrow after it.
2. **Menu.** A panel that floats above everything else on the page, with a thin frame, square corners and a soft shadow. It is never narrower than its trigger, and never wider than the window.
3. **Item.** One action per line, in <Term id="monospace">monospace</Term> and lowercase. The item under the pointer, or the one with <Term id="focus">focus</Term>, turns lighter. With keyboard focus, it also gets a bar in the focus colour on its left edge.
4. **Item icon** (optional). A small icon before the label, as decoration.

## variants and states

The trigger is an icon, or words. Use the icon on a row, where the row already says what the menu acts on. Use words where the trigger stands on its own, such as “export”.

<Example label="a trigger with an icon, and one with words, live">
  <Playground client:visible />
  <TextTrigger client:visible />
</Example>

The trigger comes in two of the heights every control shares. The small one is the usual choice, for table rows and toolbars; the medium one goes next to other medium controls.

An item can be disabled: it is dimmed, it can't be run, and the arrow keys skip it. An item can also be a danger item, for an action that deletes something or can't be undone. Its label is amber, and it stays amber under the pointer and with focus. Put danger items last.

A busy menu shows a <C slug="spinner" /> in its trigger, and it can't open. A disabled menu is off as a whole. Either way, the trigger can't take focus, so the keyboard passes over it.

## a menu of one choice

A menu can also hold a choice: one value from a few, such as a video's quality or its speed. Its items are then options, not commands, and a check marks the one that is picked. Picking another moves the check, and the choice applies at once. This variant is planned: today the menu holds commands only, and the drawings show the idea.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <OneChoiceMenu />
  <figcaption>A player's settings, behind an icon in its controls: two choices, quality and speed, each under its header, with the picked option checked.</figcaption>
</figure>

1. **Header.** The name of the choice, such as “quality” or “speed”, above its options, in <Term id="monospace">monospace</Term>, lowercase and quieter than the options. It can't be picked, and the arrow keys pass over it. A menu with only one choice can leave it out when its trigger names the choice.
2. **Check.** A small blue check before the picked option. The other options keep its place empty, so every label starts at the same point.
3. **The picked option.** Its words are the brightest grey, and a step bolder. The check says which one is picked; the words only back it.
4. **Separator.** A thin line between two choices.
5. **What auto picked** (optional). An option that leaves the pick to the system says what it picked, after a middle dot, in a quieter grey: “auto · 720p”.

Each choice is a group of its own, under its header, with exactly one option picked, so one menu can hold several choices, as a player's settings do.

### a menu of one choice, or a select

Both pick one value. They differ in where they sit, and in whose list opens.

| what | a menu of one choice | a <C slug="select" /> |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where it sits | In a toolbar or a player's controls, behind an icon or a word, among commands | In a form or a filter bar, with a visible label, among other fields |
| The value | In the menu, checked, once it is open | Always in view, in its box |
| The list that opens | The design system's menu: the same on every device, over a video too | The device's own, drawn by the browser and the operating system |
| Choices | One per group, and several groups in one menu | One |
| Options | A few, that fit without scrolling | A known list, too long to show at once |
| When it applies | At once | At once in a filter bar, or when the form is saved |

So a sort order beside the filters stays a select, and a video's quality, behind an icon in the player's controls, is a menu of one choice. For two or three options that should stay in view, show them all, as radio buttons; see [forms and settings](/design-system/patterns/forms-and-settings/).

### picking

- The menu opens on the picked option of its first choice, as a select's list opens on its value.
- The up and down arrow keys go through the options of every choice in order, past the headers and the separators, and round at the ends. <Kbd>home</Kbd> and <Kbd>end</Kbd> jump to the first option and the last.
- A click, <Kbd>enter</Kbd> or <Kbd>space</Kbd> picks the option: the check moves to it, the menu closes, focus goes back to the trigger, and the choice applies. Picking the option that is already picked only closes the menu.
- <Kbd>esc</Kbd>, <Kbd>tab</Kbd> and a press outside close the menu and change nothing, as they do for commands.
- An option can be disabled, as an item can. It is never a danger item: picking a value deletes nothing.

### names and states

- Each option tells <Term id="assistive-technology">assistive technology</Term> whether it is checked, so a <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> says which one is picked. The check itself is decoration.
- Each choice is a group named by its header, and a screen reader passes over the separator or names it as one.
- **Name the trigger after the choice:** “playback speed”, or, for a menu that holds several, after what they set together: “player settings”. Not “more”.
- The check contrasts 7.18 to 1 with the menu and 6.82 to 1 with an option under the pointer, above the 3 to 1 that <Term id="wcag">WCAG</Term> 1.4.11 asks of a part that shows a state.
- On a TV, the check marks the pick and the ring only the focus, as [TV focus](/design-system/patterns/tv-focus/#one-meaning-for-the-ring) keeps them apart.

### the words

- **Header.** A noun for the choice: “quality”, “speed”, “sort order”.
- **Options.** The values, short and written the same way, in an order that means something: from the best quality to the lowest, from the slowest speed to the fastest. Auto, when there is one, comes first. A name keeps its case.
- **Values, not verbs.** “1080p”, not “switch to 1080p”: the check shows what is picked, and the header what it is.

## behaviour

### opening and choosing

- A click on the trigger opens the menu, and another click closes it. While the menu is open, it keeps the focus.
- From the keyboard, <Kbd>enter</Kbd>, <Kbd>space</Kbd> or the down arrow key opens the menu on its first item. The up arrow key opens it on its last item.
- In the menu, the up and down arrow keys move from item to item, and go round at the ends. <Kbd>home</Kbd> and <Kbd>end</Kbd> jump to the first and the last item.
- A click, <Kbd>enter</Kbd> or <Kbd>space</Kbd> chooses an item. The menu closes and focus goes back to the trigger before the action runs. So when the item opens a <C slug="modal" />, focus comes back to the trigger once the dialog closes.
- A click on a disabled item does nothing, and the menu stays open.

### closing

- <Kbd>esc</Kbd> closes the menu and puts focus back on the trigger. In a modal, it closes only the menu; the dialog stays open.
- <Kbd>tab</Kbd> closes the menu too, and focus moves on from the trigger, as if the menu had not been there.
- A press anywhere outside the menu and its trigger closes the menu. Focus stays where that press put it.

### where it opens

- The menu opens just below its trigger. When it wouldn't fit below and there is room above, it opens above instead. It always keeps a small margin from the left and right edges of the window.
- No table cell, scrolling area or panel cuts the menu off. While it is open, it follows its trigger when anything scrolls or the window changes size, and it never flashes up in the wrong place first.

## accessibility

- **Name the thing, not the action.** An icon trigger has no words on it, so it needs a name written for it: its <Term id="accessible-name">accessible name</Term>. The browser also shows that name as a small tooltip under the pointer. Name the object the menu acts on: “actions for S04E07”, “actions for worker-2”. A table whose fifty triggers are all called “more” gives a <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> user fifty buttons they can't tell apart.
- The menu takes its name from its trigger.
- **With words on the trigger, those words are its name.** What people see is what <Term id="speech-input">speech input</Term> users say to press it.
- Danger is shown by colour only. So the label has to say what happens, such as “delete worker”. For what can't be undone, ask in a <C slug="modal" /> once the item is chosen.
- Item icons are hidden from <Term id="assistive-technology">assistive technology</Term>.

Today:

- The arrow keys skip disabled items, so people who use a keyboard never land on one. They may not learn that it exists, or why it is off. Say the reason on the page, in the row's status.
- Typing a letter doesn't move to the item that starts with it.
- A menu has no separators and no groups.
- There are no styles for <Term id="forced-colours">forced colours</Term>, the high-contrast setting some operating systems offer. A focused item shows only a fill and a bar, and forced colours remove both.

## content

- **Items.** A verb and its object, in lowercase: “pick a release”, “grab now”, “open logs”, “delete worker”.
- **Order.** The most used first, and danger last.
- **The same menu on every row.** In a list, disable an item that doesn't apply to a row rather than leaving it out, and let the row's status say why.
- **The icon trigger's name.** “actions for” and the object's name as the row shows it: “actions for S04E07”.
- **Words on a trigger.** One or two words for what is behind it: “export”.

## specs

| part | spec |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | By default a ghost `IconButton` with three 2.5px squares (`icon` replaces them): 28px square at `size="sm"`, 34px at `size="md"`. With `buttonLabel`, a default `Button` with your text and a trailing 12px chevron. |
| Menu | Rendered into `document.body` (a React portal) only while open, `position: fixed`, `z-index: 150`, so no table cell, scroll container or panel that hides its overflow clips it. `--surface` fill, a 1px `--border-2` frame, `border-radius: 0`, padding 4px 0, a shadow of `0 8px 24px` black at 45%. At least 160px wide, or as wide as the trigger if that is wider; at most the viewport's width minus 16px. |
| Item | At least 32px tall, padding 4px 12px, JetBrains Mono 13px in `--fg-2`, lowercased with `text-transform`, on one line. Under the pointer and on focus: a `--surface-2` fill and a `--fg` label. Keyboard focus adds a 2px `--focus-ring` bar inside the left edge. |
| Item icon | `icon` on an item: decoration 8px before the label, in `--fg-muted`, hidden from assistive technology. |
| Disabled item | `disabled` on an item: 45% opacity and a not-allowed cursor. It stays in the menu, can't be run, and the arrow keys skip it. |
| Danger item | `danger` on an item: the label in `--signal-amber` at rest, on hover and on focus. |
| Placement | 4px under the trigger. It opens above instead when it would come within 8px of the viewport's bottom edge and there is room above. It keeps 8px from the viewport's left and right edges, is measured before it shows, and follows the trigger on any scroll and on resize. |

| setting | in code | what it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Name | `label` | The icon trigger's accessible name, also its tooltip: “actions for S04E07”. The menu is named by its trigger. |
| Words on the trigger | `buttonLabel` | A text trigger. Its visible text is its name (WCAG 2.5.3, label in name), and `label` isn't used. |
| Size | `size="sm"` (the default), `size="md"` | The trigger's height: 28px or 34px. |
| Alignment | `align="end"` (the default), `align="start"` | The menu lines up its end edge with the trigger's, or its start edge. |
| Busy | `loading` | A spinner in the trigger, and the menu can't open. The trigger is disabled, so it can't be focused. |
| Disabled | `disabled` | The whole menu is off. The trigger is disabled, so it can't be focused. |
