# subtitles

Subtitles are read in a second, over a moving picture, sometimes from across the room. They should be plain and steady: the same look for every title, covering as little of the picture as they can, and only one at a time.

## the look

  <Do caption="White text with a dark outline, at a size that follows the picture, inside its edges.">
    <SubtitleSample look="outline" />
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="A file's own style: an oversized line on a black band, cut at both edges, and a second subtitle drawn over it.">

- **White text with a black outline,** with no box behind it. It reads on bright and dark scenes alike, and hides as little of the picture as possible.
- **Its size follows the picture,** not the screen: the same share of the picture's height on a TV, a phone or in a browser window.
- **The device's own sans-serif font,** so that every language has its letters, Chinese, Japanese and Korean included.
- **Centred at the bottom,** on at most two lines where the text allows, within 80% of the picture's width. On a TV, it stays inside the safe area, so it is never cut at an edge.
- **One look for every title.** Colours, boxes, fonts and positions that come inside a file are ignored.
- **One subtitle at a time.**

### people's own settings

Phones and TVs have caption settings of their own, under accessibility. When someone has set them, for example a box behind the text because that reads better for them, their settings win over ours. Our look is the default for everyone else.

## the menu

<SubtitleMenu />

- **“off” comes first.**
- **Then the languages that matter here:** the person's preferred subtitle language, then the other subtitle languages the server is set up for.
- **Then “more languages”,** when a file brings others. It opens the rest, so a long list never buries the few people use.
- **Within a language,** the plain track comes first, then “for deaf and hard of hearing”, then “foreign-language parts only”.
- **Names follow the word list:** “English · for deaf and hard of hearing”, “Chinese (Simplified)”, “Portuguese (Brazil)”.
- **Two tracks of the same language and kind** show once.

## what switches on by itself

- **Nothing in another language** than the one people chose.
- **Their preferred subtitle language,** when they set one in settings and the title has it.
- **“Foreign-language parts only” in the language people listen in,** because it translates the lines the audio doesn't.

## specs

- **The look.** Text `#FFFFFF`; edge: outline, `#000000`; background and window: transparent; size: 5.33% of the picture's height; font: the system's sans-serif.
- **Android (Media3).** A subtitle view with embedded styles and embedded font sizes switched off, a fractional text size of 0.0533, and that caption style. When the system's caption settings are switched on, it takes the user's style and text size instead. On a TV, the subtitle view is inset by the safe area.
- **Web.** The same through `::cue`: white, no background, an outline drawn with text shadows, and a font size relative to the video's height.
- **The list.** Built from the tracks the title has, ordered as above; the server's subtitle languages come from its configuration. A packaged title carries only those, so it has no “more languages”.
