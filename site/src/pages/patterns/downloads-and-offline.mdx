# downloads and offline

On a phone or a tablet, people can download a title and watch it without a connection: on a train, on a plane, wherever the signal drops. A download is a copy on the device, with its subtitles, kept for the account that made it, and it plays even when the server can't be reached.

Every title says plainly whether it's on the phone, on its way, or can't come along, and how much room it takes. This page says “phone” throughout; on a tablet, the app says “tablet”.

## one status per title

The download control sits next to “play” on a title's page. It shows the title's download status, and offers the one thing that makes sense in it.

<DownloadStates />

| status | the control says | what people can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not on the phone | “download · 1.4 GB” | Download it |
| Waiting its turn | “queued” | Cancel |
| Waiting for Wi-Fi | “waiting for Wi-Fi” | Cancel; it starts by itself on Wi-Fi |
| Waiting for a connection | “waiting for a connection” | Cancel; it starts by itself once the phone is online |
| On its way | “downloading 42%”, with a thin bar | Cancel |
| On the phone | “downloaded · 1.4 GB” | Play it, delete it |
| Needs a check | “downloaded · check needed”, and why: “Downloads are checked with the server every 30 days. This one plays again once the server can be reached.” | Nothing until the server can be reached; then it plays |
| Failed | “couldn't download”, and why in a sentence: “Not enough space on this phone.”, “Can't reach the server.” | “try again”, or delete what was started |
| Can't come along | “can't be downloaded”, and why: “This title can only be streamed.” | Nothing |

- The size is the size of exactly what will be downloaded at the chosen quality, as the server reports it, so it is the same number before and after the download. When it isn't known, the control leaves it out rather than guess.
- Status and size are one text: “queued · 347 MB”, “waiting for Wi-Fi · 281 MB”, “downloading 15% · 281 MB”, “downloaded · 1.4 GB”. When it wraps, it wraps as one, and “check needed” stays together.
- The percentage changes at most once a second, so it reads calmly.
- “Waiting for Wi-Fi” appears only when downloads are set to Wi-Fi only and the phone has none. It is true, and it says what happens next.
- The reason of a failure is a reason, not an instruction: the control already offers “try again”, so the sentence doesn't repeat it.
- Nothing here promises a feature that isn't built: a title that can't be downloaded says so, without a “yet”.

### series

Each episode has its own control. A season offers all of its episodes at once, with their number and total size: “download season 1 · 8 episodes · 9.6 GB”, but only when every episode of the season can be downloaded and none is on the phone or on its way yet. When some can only be streamed, it offers the ones that can: “download 6 episodes · 7.2 GB”. Once some of them are on the phone or on their way, it offers the rest, and says so: “download 2 more episodes · 724 MB”. A series that is partly on the phone says how far: “3 of 8 downloaded”.

When the server can't be reached, a series' page shows what is on the phone, without a total it can't know: “2 episodes on this phone”, and no check mark while one of them needs a check.

## whose downloads they are

A download belongs to the account that made it. Another profile on the same phone doesn't see it, so a child's profile never plays a film meant for a parent. When a sign-in expires, nothing is hidden: signing in again as the same account brings everything back. With several accounts on the phone and no server to ask, the app opens on the account picker first, then on that account's downloads.

Signing out, removing an account from the phone and changing the server delete that account's downloads, so the app asks first and names what goes:

| action | dialog title | it says | buttons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sign out | “sign out” | “Your 23 downloads, 12.4 GB, are deleted from this phone.” | “cancel”, “sign out” |
| Remove an account | “remove account” | “The 23 downloads of Anna, 12.4 GB, are deleted from this phone.” | “cancel”, “remove account” |
| Change the server | “change server” | “Your 23 downloads, 12.4 GB, are deleted from this phone.” | “cancel”, “change server” |

The confirming button repeats the title and is the danger button. When the account has no downloads, the action works as it always does.

A download can also go because the server says so: when the account may no longer watch a title, because it left the library or a profile's limit changed, its download is deleted. The app says so once, in words that are true in both cases: “This title isn't available to this account anymore, so it was deleted from this phone.” When several go at once, a banner on the downloads screen says it for all of them, with their titles under details:

<Banner state="information" live="off" details="Paper Station S01E02, Paper Station S01E03, Quiet Orchard">3 downloads were deleted from this phone: they aren't available to this account anymore.</Banner>

## the downloads screen

The downloads screen lists everything on the phone, newest first, grouped by series and season, with movies in a group of their own. Each title shows its size and its status, and plays from there. A title that failed shows its reason and no size, because nothing of it is on the phone.

At the top, one line says how much room downloads take, and how much is left:

<StorageLine />

- **Delete one.** From the title's menu or its control. It asks nothing, because the title can be downloaded again.
- **Delete all.** One action at the end of the list. It asks once, in a dialog titled with what it does, “delete all downloads”, and names what goes: “This removes 23 titles, 12.4 GB, from this phone.”, with “delete all” and “cancel”. It counts only what is on the phone.
- **Empty.** “nothing downloaded yet”, and “Titles you download show up here, ready to watch without a connection.”

## when the server can't be reached

When the app can't reach the server, it opens on the downloads screen, with a banner:

<Banner state="information" live="off">Can't reach the server. Your downloads still play.</Banner>

That sentence is true whether the phone has no connection or the server is down, and it says what still works. When nothing is downloaded, the banner says “Can't reach the server.” alone, because nothing else would be true.

- **A downloaded title's page** is built from what is on the phone, with the banner on top, under the top bar. What needs the server is left out: the watchlist, lists, the watched mark, the trailer and the people. A title that needs its check shows “play” or “resume” dimmed, with the sentence that says why.
- **The player** offers the subtitles that came with the download, by the same names as online. A title without subtitles offers only “off”.
- **Every other screen** that needs the server shows the usual “couldn't load” screen, with the way out that fits the cause.
- **Watch progress** made without a connection is kept on the phone and sent once the server can be reached again, with nothing for people to do. When the server is back, the banner goes, and queued downloads carry on.

## settings

- **Download quality.** The same names as the player's quality menu, each with its size for an hour of video, as the server reports it: “720p · about 932 MB an hour”. Where too few titles have a quality to tell its size reliably, the size is left out.
- **Download over Wi-Fi only.** On by default, so a download never uses mobile data unless people want it to.

## words

- Say “downloads” and “on this phone”, or “on this tablet” on a tablet. Avoid “offline mode”, “sync” and “cache”: they describe the app, not what people get.
- Use the app's own words for what it holds: “movies”, as its tab says.
- Sizes in GB with one decimal, without a trailing zero, and in MB below 1 GB: “1.4 GB”, “18 GB”, “820 MB”.
- A failure says why, in a sentence people can act on: “Not enough space on this phone.”, “The server didn't accept the download.” The technical reason goes under details.

## specs

- **States** follow the download manager's own: queued, waiting for the network, downloading, completed, failed and removing. Removing shows nothing: the row goes at once.
- **Waiting** is only for the network, and for Wi-Fi when that setting is on. Downloads don't wait for a charger: that would keep a background service and its notification running for hours.
- **“Can't be downloaded”** is for titles without a downloadable package. The server says which titles can be downloaded.
- **Accounts.** Downloads are stored per account. Signing out, removing an account and changing the server delete that account's downloads, after the question above.
- **The 30-day check.** A download that hasn't been checked with the server for 30 days asks for the server before it plays. With the server reachable, the check runs without anything on screen. When the server says the account may no longer watch a title, its download is deleted.
- **Subtitles.** A download takes all of the title's subtitle tracks with it; they are a few kilobytes each.
- **Space.** A failed download frees what it had downloaded so far; a waiting or queued one keeps it, to carry on. The storage line and the questions compute one total, from the bytes, rounded once.
- **Progress** in whole percent, updated at most once a second.
- **Watch progress** made offline is stored with its time and sent in order once the server can be reached. The server's rule for a finished title applies to it like to any other.
- **The banner** uses the information state, without a close button while the server can't be reached.
- **Tablet** from 600 dp of width: “on this tablet”.
