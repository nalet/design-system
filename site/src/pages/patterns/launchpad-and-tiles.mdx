# launchpad & tiles

The <Term id="launchpad">launchpad</Term> is the first page people see when they sign in to the <Term id="portal">portal</Term>. It has one job: to take people to the right <Term id="app">app</Term> quickly, and to show them where something needs their attention.

It does that with <Term id="tile">tiles</Term>, one for each app, sorted into a few <Term id="tile-group">groups</Term> that always appear in the same order.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <LaunchpadOverview />
  <figcaption>A launchpad: a search at the top, then three groups in a fixed order. The amber 3 is a number tile: three things that need attention.</figcaption>
</figure>

The launchpad, the tile and the tile group are real components. Their React pages show them live, in every variant: the [launchpad](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/patterns-portal--docs), the [tile](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-navigation-tile--docs) and the [tile group](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-navigation-tile-group--docs).

## one page, in a fixed order

People find things by where they were last time. So the launchpad is a single page, and its groups and tiles keep their places from one visit to the next: apps first, then manage, then settings. After a few visits, people reach for a tile without reading.

One page works well for up to about 30 tiles. Beyond that, or when different audiences need a different start, such as the people who run the platform and the people who only use it, separate home pages for each audience become worth it. That is the way to grow; until then, one page is simpler for everyone.

## search finds the tile

When people know what they want, typing is quicker than looking. Typing on the launchpad narrows the tiles in place: tiles that don't match step aside, and so does every group left with nothing in it. When nothing matches at all, the page says so, and how to get back.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <SearchNarrows />
  <figcaption>Typing “down” narrows the launchpad to the tiles that match. Groups with nothing left step aside until the search is cleared.</figcaption>
</figure>

A page that only holds a few number tiles, as a board of key figures, can leave the search out.

Later, the search in the <Term id="shell-bar">shell bar</Term> will find apps from anywhere, not only from the launchpad. This is planned.

A catalogue that lists every app to browse through isn't needed for now. It becomes useful once people can arrange pages of their own, to pick the apps they want on them.

## favourites, later

Most people use the same two or three apps every day. Later, each person will be able to pin those to a “my apps” group at the top of the launchpad, so that they always come first. This is planned.

## when something gets a tile

A tile should lead somewhere worth going on its own. If everything gets a tile, the launchpad turns into a second menu that competes with the apps' own navigation, and it grows every time someone adds a screen. So before adding a tile, ask what it opens.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <WhatGetsATile />
  <figcaption>What gets a tile, and what doesn't.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **An app with its own job gets one tile**, in the group of the people who use it. The tile opens the app where the app opens: on what needs attention.
- **A number someone must act on may get a <Term id="number-tile">number tile</Term>**, one per app at most. A number tile that says “needs attention 3” opens the app, showing just those three. The number turns amber or red only when it means attention; otherwise it stays neutral.
- **A frequent task with its own way in may get a <Term id="task-tile">task tile</Term>**, but rarely. Requesting a title is one: people do it often, and it has its own entry point.
- **An app's <Term id="view">views</Term>, its setup screens and links to other places get no tile.** Views belong in the app's own [navigation](/design-system/patterns/app-navigation/), setup belongs in [settings](/design-system/patterns/settings-and-setup/), and a link to somewhere else belongs inside an app.

## groups

Groups help people scan: they look for their group first, then for the tile in it. That only works when there are few groups, always in the same place, named after the people who use them.

- **Group by who uses the tiles, and why.** The apps group is for everyone's daily work, manage for the people who look after the library, and settings for the people who may change the platform. Not by addon or by vendor: people don't know, and shouldn't need to know, where an app came from.
- **About four groups at most, always in the same order.** Today that is apps, manage and settings; once favourites arrive, “my apps” goes on top.
- **An addon joins an existing group.** It doesn't bring a group of its own.
- **A group with nothing a person may open is hidden.** People only see the groups and tiles they can use.
- **The same group names in every installation**, so people who use more than one feel at home, and guides and screenshots stay true.

## what an addon brings

An <Term id="addon">addon</Term> is an app that is installed into the platform on its own. It brings one app tile and, if it helps, one number tile, and both join a group that already exists, usually the one for the people who will use the app.

## the tile

A tile is a square that opens an app. Pressing anywhere on it opens the app, so it is one large, easy target. There are two kinds: an app tile, with the app's icon, its name and a few words about it, and a number tile, with a number and how it has changed.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <TileAnatomy />
  <figcaption>An app tile and a number tile, close up.</figcaption>
</figure>

1. **Icon.** The app's line icon, in blue. It is decoration: the name says what the tile is.
2. **Name.** The app's name, one or two words, without a verb. Long names are cut to one line, so keep it short.
3. **Description.** One line on what the app is for, in sentence case. It stops after two lines.
4. **Badge** (optional). A small label at the top right: how many things wait, like 3, or one word for a state, like “new”, “failed” or “needs setup”.
5. **Status dot** (optional). A small square that shows how the app is doing: green, amber or red. Use it only when it changes what people do, and put the state into words in the description too, because on screen the dot is colour only.
6. **The number** (number tiles). The figure, sometimes with its unit, and how it changed since last time, with its sign: +2, −11.

Tiles come in three sizes, small, medium and large; medium is the usual one.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <TileStates />
  <figcaption>When people point at a tile or reach it with the keyboard, it lights up and a small chevron appears after its name. The current app's tile has a blue bar on its left edge. A tile that can't be opened is faded.</figcaption>
</figure>

A tile can also open another site in a new tab; today nothing on the tile shows that, so say it in the description. And a tile that only shows a figure, with nowhere to go, doesn't react to pointing and can't be reached with the Tab key.

## tile groups

A tile group gathers tiles under a name that stays put. It is what the launchpad is made of, and it also works on its own: a short set of places to go at the end of a page, or a row of number tiles as key figures. A single tile doesn't need a group around it.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <GroupAnatomy />
  <figcaption>A tile group, close up.</figcaption>
</figure>

1. **Title.** The group's name, led by a small blue chevron: apps, manage, settings. It is a heading, so people can jump to it.
2. **Action** (optional). One quiet button for the group as a whole, at the end of the title row. Leave out the loud, blue kind: it would compete with the tiles.
3. **Description** (optional). One short line under the title, only when the name needs it.
4. **Tiles.** In a grid with as many columns as there is room for, so the grid reflows when the screen gets narrower.

  <Do caption="On a phone, the grid fits fewer, wider columns, and every name stays readable.">
    <GridReflows />
  </Do>

  </Dont>
</DoDont>

When a search hides every tile in a group, the whole group steps aside with them.

## using it with a keyboard

Pressing Tab through every tile on a busy launchpad would take a long time. So all the tiles together are one stop for the Tab key, and the arrow keys move between them, across groups.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <ArrowKeys />
  <figcaption>Tab reaches the tiles once; the arrow keys then move from tile to tile, and down into the next group.</figcaption>
</figure>

| key | what it does |
| --- | --- |
| Tab | Moves onto the tiles, landing on the tile people were last on, or on the first one. The next Tab moves on past the tiles. |
| → and ← | The next or the previous tile, in reading order, across groups. It stops at the first and the last tile. |
| ↓ and ↑ | The tile below or above on the screen, crossing into the next group. |
| Home and End | The first or the last tile. |
| Enter | Opens the tile. Space presses a tile that is a button rather than a link. |
| / | From among the tiles, jumps to the search. |
| Esc, in the search | Clears the search and moves to the first tile. |

Tiles that can't be opened, and the tiles of a group that steps aside, are skipped. An arrow key pressed on something else among the tiles, such as a group's action, jumps to the first tile. A tile group on its own works the same way. A launchpad can also give each group a Tab stop of its own, and when people get stuck with the arrow keys, every tile can be its own Tab stop instead.

## accessibility

What makes the launchpad work for everyone, including people who use a <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term>:

- The launchpad's title is the page's main heading, and each group's title is a heading under it, so people can jump from group to group. The search is a region of its own that they can jump to as well.
- While people search, a screen reader says how many apps match. When nothing matches, the message is visible text.
- A tile is read as one thing: its name, its badge, its status, then its description or its number. Keep each part short, because people hear all of it.
- The icon, the chevron and the small triangle beside a change are decoration. The status dot is read out, but on screen it is colour only, so the description says the state in words.
- Write the sign into a change, like +2 or −11, so it is read out, not only drawn.
- The current app's tile is marked as the current page for screen readers too, not only by its blue bar.

Today:

- The arrow keys don't work inside the search field. Tab from the search reaches the first visible tile, and the arrows work from there.
- Esc in the search only reaches the first tile when that tile's group was visible during the search; otherwise the <Term id="focus">focus</Term> stays in the search.
- The count a screen reader hears, and the default message when nothing matches, are in English only, and with one match it says “1 app match”.
- Nothing tells screen-reader users about the arrow keys, and a screen reader's reading mode still reads every tile.
- The search field's frame is fainter than other text fields' frames, and its focus is a blue frame rather than the usual focus outline.
- The launchpad listens for / and the arrow keys anywhere among its tiles, so a text field placed in a group's title row can't receive a /. Keep text fields out of that area.
- The title is always the page's main heading, and a group's title always the level below it, whatever headings are around them.
- A tile that can't be opened is read as plain text, not as a link that is unavailable. If the reason matters, put it in the description.
- A badge with a count is read as a bare number. Say what it counts in the description.

## the words

- **The launchpad's title.** The name of the place, in one word: “launchpad”, “home”. No greeting and no tagline; people skip them on every visit, and they push the tiles down.
- **The search.** Its placeholder says what it finds, “search apps…”, in the same words as its name.
- **Group titles.** Who the tiles are for, in one or two words, always the same: apps, manage, settings. Avoid “misc”, “other”, or one group for everything.
- **Tile names.** The app's name, as a noun rather than a verb: “downloads”, not “open downloads”.
- **Descriptions.** What the app is for, in a few words, in sentence case: “What is on its way”.
- **Badges.** A count, like 3, or one word: “new”, “failed”, “needs setup”. Not “!”, which says that something is wrong but not what.
- **Numbers.** The figure and its unit, rounded to what people can act on, and a change with its sign.
- **When nothing matches.** What didn't match, and what to do, for example: *No apps match “backup”. Try another word, or press Esc in the search box to clear it.*

| Title bar | The title and the search in one row, 16px apart, wrapping on narrow screens; without a title, the search sits alone at the right end |
| Search field | 32px high; mono 13px; `--surface` fill; 1px `--border-2`; placeholder in `--fg-dim`; 16rem wide, growing to 24rem |

| Tile | Square; 1px `--border`; `--surface` fill; 16px padding, 12px between rows; at least 7rem (112px) tall. Small: 5.5rem, 12px padding, 8px rows. Large: 9rem |
| Tile icon | A 22px line icon with a 1.75 stroke, `--cloud-blue`, in a 28px box |
| Tile name | JetBrains Mono 600, 14px, `--fg`, lowercase, one line with an ellipsis; a 14px chevron after it on hover and focus |
| Tile description | Inter 13px, line-height 1.45, `--fg-muted`, at most two lines |
| Badge | 18px high, mono 11px, top right; a number gets tabular figures. Tones: info `--cloud-blue`, success `--signal-green`, warning `--signal-amber`, danger `--signal-red`, neutral `--fg-muted` on `--surface-2` |
| Status dot | An 8px square: online `--signal-green`, degraded `--signal-amber`, offline `--signal-red`, unknown `--fg-dim` |
| Number | The value in mono 700, 28px; the unit 14px in `--fg-muted`; the change in mono 12px with a small triangle: positive `--signal-green`, negative `--signal-red`, neutral `--fg-muted`; pinned to the bottom of the tile |
