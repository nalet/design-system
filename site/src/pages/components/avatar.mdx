# avatar

An avatar is the small square that stands for a person: their picture, or their initials when there is no picture. In the top bar, it is the button that opens the user menu; [top bar](/design-system/patterns/top-bar/) explains why every product shows the person the same way.

<Example label="an avatar with a picture, and one with initials">
  <Picture />
</Example>

## when to use

- For the person who is signed in, at the end of the top bar. The <C slug="top-bar">user menu</C> draws it there, as its button.
- Beside a person's name, where a list or a page shows who did something or who owns it.

## when to use something else

- **For an app or a thing.** An app has its icon, on its tile; an avatar is always a person.
- **For a state or a count.** Use a <C slug="status" /> or a <C slug="badge" />.
- **For a button of its own.** An avatar alone can't be pressed. The user menu makes it one; elsewhere, put it inside a link or a button that has a name.

## anatomy

1. **Square.** A thin frame around a slightly lighter fill, with square corners, like every frame in the system.
2. **Picture.** It fills the square and is cropped to it from its middle, never squeezed.
3. **Initials.** In place of the picture: one or two letters, in <Term id="monospace">monospace</Term> and lowercase.

## variants and states

An avatar comes in the three heights every control has, so it lines up with a button or a field beside it. Medium is the usual size, and the one in the top bar.

<Example label="three sizes">
  <Sizes />
</Example>

The initials are the first letter of the first and of the last word of the person's name; a name of one word gives one letter. Where those aren't the person's initials, as with a title before the name, give the initials yourself.

<Example label="a title in the name gives the wrong letters; initials of its own put it right">
  <OwnInitials />
</Example>

When the picture can't be loaded, the initials show instead, so the square is never empty.

Beside the person's name, the avatar is decoration: the name already says who it is.

<Example label="an avatar beside the person's name">
  <BesideAName />
</Example>

## behaviour

- An avatar doesn't take <Term id="focus">focus</Term>, and nothing happens when it is pressed.
- On a TV, its initials are never smaller than a label; see [layout](/design-system/foundations/layout/#touch-and-tv-sizes).

## accessibility

- **On its own, it is a picture named after the person.** A <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> says the person's name, not the initials.
- **Beside the name, or inside a button with a name, it is decoration**, and <Term id="assistive-technology">assistive technology</Term> skips it, so the name isn't read twice.
- **The initials are text**, in the brightest text colour on the square's fill, so they keep their <Term id="contrast">contrast</Term> and grow with the reader's text size.

Today:

- A picture is the person's own, and nothing checks what it shows or how well it reads at this size.

## content

- **The name.** The person's name as they write it: “Ada Urban”. It is what a screen reader says, so don't give an email address or a user id where there is a name.
- **The initials.** Lowercase, like all monospace in the system: “au”. Two letters at most read well in the small size.

## specs

| size | in code | square | initials |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | `size="sm"` | 28px | 12px |
| Medium | `size="md"` (the default) | 34px | 13px |
| Large | `size="lg"` | 42px | 14px |

| part | spec |
| --- | --- |
| Square | A 1px `--border-control` frame on `--surface-2`, `border-radius: 0` |
| Picture | `src`. An `<img>` that fills the square, cropped with `object-fit: cover`, with an empty `alt`: the avatar carries the name. When it fails to load, the initials show |
| Initials | `initials`, or the first letter of the first and of the last word of `name`. JetBrains Mono 600 in `--fg`, lowercase |
| Name | `name`. On its own, the avatar is `role="img"` named by it |
| Decoration | `decorative`: `aria-hidden`, with no role and no name |
| On a tv | Under `data-nc-screen="tv"`, the initials are 14px at every size |

Other attributes and the `ref` reach the square.
