# data table

The interactive table: sorting, row selection, collapsible groups, growing, pop-in on narrow widths, and one tab stop with arrow-key row focus.

  <Playground client:visible />
</Example>

## when to use

- For a list people work through: scan it, sort it, select rows and act on them. It is the body of a [worklist](/design-system/patterns/worklist/).
- When rows need any of: sorting by a column, selecting one or many, grouping under header rows, loading in pages ("show 100 more"), moving between rows from the keyboard.
- For long lists. With `growing` it stays fast at thousands of rows — see performance on its [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-data-display-data-table--docs).

## when not to use

- **For a short, read-only set of records.** Use the <C slug="table" />: native markup, no focus model, no row state. It costs less and announces less.
- **For the fields of one record.** One row with many columns reads badly. List label and value pairs in a <C slug="card" />.
- **For destinations.** A set of apps or areas to open is a <C slug="tile-group" />, or the [launchpad](/design-system/patterns/launchpad-and-tiles/).
- **To pick values in a form.** Use <C slug="checkbox">checkboxes</C> or a <C slug="multi-select" />. Row selection is for acting on rows, not a form value.
- **When every column must stay a column at any width.** The data table never scrolls sideways; it pops columns in. The <C slug="table" /> keeps its columns and scrolls inside its frame.

## anatomy

1. **Frame** — 1px `--border` on every side, `--surface`, full width. It never scrolls, so the header can stick to the page; a narrow table pops columns in instead.
2. **Header row** — a header per column on `--bg-2`: JetBrains Mono 600, 11px, letter-spacing 0.04em, `--fg-muted`, lowercase, one line. Padding 8px 12px, a 1px `--border-2` rule below. It sticks while the page scrolls.
3. **Sort button** (sortable columns) — the label and a 10 × 12px glyph of two chevrons: the sorted direction drawn full, the other at 35%. On hover, and on the sorted column, the label turns `--fg`. Keyboard focus draws a 2px `--focus-ring` outline.
4. **Select column** (`selectable`) — 32px wide, holding the 16px <C slug="checkbox" />: "select every visible row" in the header, "select …" in each row.
5. **Group row** (grouped) — one cell across every column on `--bg-2`, padding 6px 12px, at least 28px tall, laid out as three tracks with 8px between them: a 12px caret in `--fg-muted` (pointing down while open, right while collapsed), the label, and the group action at the end. The label is mono 600 12px in `--fg`, written as it is (not lowercased), on one line cut with an ellipsis; on a table 480px wide or less it wraps instead. Its track can shrink, so a long name never makes the table wider.
6. **Data row** — cells with padding 8px 12px and a 1px `--border` rule below, 13px in `--fg-2`, vertically centred. The cells take the page's font; the <C slug="table-cells" /> set their own. Hover tints a row `--surface-2`; a selected row is `--surface-selected` with a 2px `--cloud-blue` rule on its leading edge.
7. **Row header** — the first column, or the one marked as the row header, in `--fg`: what names the row. Popped-in values sit under its content.
8. **Highlight** (highlighted rows) — a 3px rule in the state's colour (`--state-error`, `--state-warning`, …) on the leading edge. On a selected row it takes the place of the blue selection rule.
9. **Pop-ins** (narrow) — label and value pairs under the row header's content, 4px below it and 2px apart: the label mono 11px in `--fg-dim`, the value 12px in `--fg-muted`, 8px after it. A pair that doesn't fit wraps.
10. **Empty cell** (no rows) — one cell across every column, with no padding of its own; its content brings it.
11. **Growing footer** (`growing`) — the count (mono 12px, `--fg-muted`, tabular figures) and a small "show n more" <C slug="button" />, centred, padding 12px, on `--bg-2` with a 1px `--border` rule above.
12. **Progress rule** (`loading`) — a 2px `--cloud-blue` bar, 30% wide, moving along the frame's top edge.

## variants and states

### sorting

- A click on the sorted column's header flips its direction. A click on another header sorts that column in its first direction: ascending, unless the column starts descending — dates usually do. There is no third click back to unsorted.
- The sort is stable: rows that compare equal keep their order. Text compares naturally ("E2" before "E10") and ignores case and accents, dates compare by time, and empty values go last in either direction.
- Grouped rows keep their groups: groups appear in the order their first row has after sorting.

### selection

A selectable table has a checkbox column. What selecting is for — when to offer it, press versus select, select all, keeping a selection — is the [selection and bulk actions](/design-system/patterns/selection-and-bulk-actions/) pattern. What the table does today:

- People select with a row's checkbox, a click on the row (when a press doesn't open the row), `space` on a focused row, ranges with `shift`, and `ctrl+a` / `cmd+a` — see behaviour, below.
- The header checkbox means **every visible row**: the rows on screen, not those in collapsed groups or past the growing limit. It is checked while every visible row that can be selected is selected, and mixed (indeterminate) while anything else is selected. Pressed while checked, it clears the whole selection, hidden rows included. It is disabled while no visible row can be selected. Offering "select all 2,500" is the <C slug="toolbar">selection toolbar</C>'s job.
- A row that can't be selected has a disabled checkbox, and is kept out of clicks, `space`, ranges, select-all and `ctrl+a`. Its status says why — see [rows that can't be selected](/design-system/patterns/selection-and-bulk-actions/#rows-that-cant-be-selected).

### grouping

- Rows that share a group go under one group row, whose label says what the group is and how many it holds: "The Example Show · S04 · 8 missing". The label keeps its case.
- A group row can carry one action at its end: a small ghost <C slug="button" /> that acts on the group ("grab all 8"). Pressing it does not toggle the group.
- A group collapses and expands with a click on its row, `enter` or `space`, `←` and `→`.

</Example>

### growing

- A growing table shows a page of rows, 100 for instance. A "show 100 more" button under the table adds as many again ("show 37 more" for the last ones) and goes once everything is shown.
- The count beside it reads "100 of 2,500": the data rows shown, of every row. Each step is announced.
- What counts as shown: data rows on screen. Group rows don't count. Rows in a collapsed group count in the total but never as shown, so with groups collapsed the count can stop below the total ("2,380 of 2,500") with no button left.
- The limit only grows, until the view changes: a new filter or another tab can start it over at one page.

### pop-in

- Pop-in goes by the table's own width, not the screen's: a table in a narrow panel pops in on a wide screen too. Columns of `low` importance pop in when the table is 720px wide or less, `medium` at 480px or less. `high` columns, and columns with no importance given, always show, and the row header never pops in.
- A popped-in column is not shown as a column at all: no header, no cells. Its values move under the row header's content, each with its label.
- The table never scrolls sideways. Keep the `high` columns few enough to fit the narrowest width you support.
- Today: when the sorted column pops in, its header goes with it. The order holds, but nothing on screen or in the accessibility tree says what the rows are sorted by.

### row press

- A row can itself be an action, usually opening its details: a click outside the row's controls, or `enter` on the focused row, presses it. The checkbox, `space`, `shift+space` and `shift+↑` / `shift+↓` still select.
- Without a row press, a click on a selectable row toggles its selection, and `enter` does nothing.
- How a press and a selection share a row is [press versus select](/design-system/patterns/selection-and-bulk-actions/#press-versus-select).
- Today: a pressable row looks like any other — no pointer cursor, no chevron — and nothing tells assistive tech that `enter` opens it. Put a link in the row header as well, so where the row leads is a real link people can find.

### highlight

- A row can carry a state — `error`, `warning`, `information`, `success` or `neutral` — as a 3px rule in that state's colour on its leading edge. The example at the top highlights the rows that failed.
- The rule is decoration: it tells assistive tech nothing, and colour alone never carries a state. The row says the state in words, in a <C slug="table-cells">status cell</C>.
- Use it for the few rows that need acting on (`error` for what failed), not to colour every row by its state.

### sticky header

- The header sticks by default: while the rows scroll under it, it stays at the top of the page — or, inside a panel with its own scroll, at the top of that panel.
- Under something else that sticks — an app bar, a sticky toolbar — the header is offset to stick below it. The [worklist](/design-system/patterns/worklist/)'s header sticks below its toolbar.

### loading

- While it loads, a 2px `--cloud-blue` rule runs along the table's top edge (a still, full-width rule with reduced motion). The rows stay at full contrast, and stay interactive.
- Keep the old rows while new ones load; don't empty the table first.
- Show the empty state only once you know the list is empty, not while it loads.
- The rule sits on the frame's top edge and doesn't stick with the header: in a long list it can be scrolled out of view.

- With no rows, the table shows its empty state in its body, across every column. Which kind, and what it says, is [empty states](/design-system/patterns/empty-states/).
- Without one, an empty table is a header row with nothing under it.

## behaviour

- **One tab stop.** The table keeps one row in the tab order: the last one focused, or the first row to begin with. The controls inside every other row are out of the tab order, so `tab` crosses the table in one step instead of stopping at each checkbox and menu.
- `tab` from the focused row goes into that row's own controls (its checkbox, its action menu), then out of the table to the "show n more" button or beyond. The header's controls — select-all and the sort buttons — stay in the tab order before the rows; `shift+tab` reaches them.
- The arrow keys move from row to row, group rows included; `space` toggles the focused row's selection and `enter` presses it; on a group row, `←` and `→` collapse and expand it.
- A click on a row focuses it, so it becomes the tab stop.
- The focused row draws a 2px `--focus-ring` frame inside its cells, for keyboard focus only.
- When the row that has focus leaves the list (an action removed it, a filter hid it), or the last "show n more" press removes its button, focus moves to the table's tab stop — the row last focused, or the first row — instead of falling to the page.

With a pointer:

- A click on a row toggles its selection, or presses the row when a press opens it. `shift+click` on a checkbox — or on the row, when a press doesn't open it — toggles that row and sets every row from the last one toggled the same way.
- A click on a group row toggles the group.
- Clicks on a row's own controls — checkbox, buttons, links, menus — are theirs. They never toggle or press the row.
- A click while text is selected on the page does nothing, so people can copy from a row. A `shift+click` is the exception: it selects the range of rows, and the table keeps the browser from selecting text on it.

The keyboard map and the attributes it sets are on its [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-data-display-data-table--docs).

## accessibility

- A screen reader names each cell by its column and its row: the headers are real column headers, and the row header names the row.
- **Name the table**, by the heading above it or with a label of its own. Nothing enforces it.
- **Name the rows.** Each checkbox is named by its row, "select S04E07"; name the row's <C slug="action-menu" /> the same way, "actions for S04E07".
- An actions column needs a header too: a visually hidden one, "actions".
- **States in words.** A highlight rule tells assistive tech nothing; a <C slug="table-cells">status cell</C> says the state.
- Announcements: each growing step is announced by its count. A sort is told on its header, not announced. The table doesn't announce selection changes; a <C slug="toolbar">selection toolbar</C> above it does ("8 selected").

Today:

- The arrow keys move by row only. A cell can't take focus, so `←` and `→` don't move across a row's cells, and the header row is outside the arrow keys (reach it with `shift+tab`).
- The built-in words are fixed English: "select every visible row", the "select" before each row's label, and "show n more".
- A load starting or finishing is not announced. When it takes long, say so in words near the table.
- `ctrl+a` / `cmd+a` on a group row selects the page's text: the table takes it on data rows only.

## content

- **Headers** — a lowercase noun, one or two words: `episode`, `aired`, `state`. Put a unit in the header, `size (gb)`, not in every cell.
- **The row header** — what people call the row, `S04E07` or `worker-2`, in an <C slug="table-cells">identifier cell</C>. Name the row's checkbox and its action menu with the same words, so the checkbox, the action menu and the row agree.
- **Status** — one status cell per row, from one table of the object's states (see [status vocabulary](/design-system/patterns/status-vocabulary/)): the state in the object's words, and a detail line for why or what happens next.
- **Dates** — a <C slug="table-cells">date cell</C>: absolute and relative.
- **Group headers** — what the group is and how many it holds: `The Example Show · S04 · 8 missing`. A group's name is content: the label shows it as written, so write it the way the name is spelled.
- **Group actions** — a verb and the count it acts on: `grab all 8`.
- **Growing** — keep the "100 of 2,500" form. Change its words for another language, not for other words.
- **Empty** — what is true, in the list's own words: see [empty states](/design-system/patterns/empty-states/).
