# rail

When a product takes a rail, what it holds on each screen and how its views are named is on the guidelines site: **[rail guidance →](https://nalet.github.io/design-system/components/rail/)** and **[consumer navigation →](https://nalet.github.io/design-system/patterns/consumer-navigation/)**

<Canvas of={RailStories.Playground} />

## examples

`views` are the product's views, each with an `href`, a `name` and an `icon` at 24. `mark` draws the product's mark at the rail's head, and `foot` puts settings at its foot. `current` is the `href` of the current view. The stories take every press over in `onNavigate`, as a router would.

```tsx
<Rail
  mark={{ href: '/', product: 'video' }}
  views={[
    { href: '/', name: 'home', icon: <Icon icon={House} size={24} /> },
    { href: '/films', name: 'films', icon: <Icon icon={Film} size={24} /> },
  ]}
  foot={{ href: '/settings', name: 'settings', icon: <Icon icon={Settings} size={24} /> }}
  current="/films"
  onNavigate={(event, href) => {
    event.preventDefault();
    router.push(href);
  }}
/>
```

Without `current`, no view is the current one: a screen that belongs to none of them, such as a film's page.

<Canvas of={RailStories.NoneCurrent} />

Inside `data-nc-screen="touch"`, it is the rail of a phone held sideways: the tabs span the rail and sit in the middle of its height, and the current one has no lighter square. Pass the phone's tabs as `views`, and neither `mark` nor `foot`.

<Canvas of={RailStories.Sideways} />

Inside `data-nc-screen="tv"`, the rail holds the frame: `head` takes the person and the search, and `foot` the notifications and settings. It opens while the focus is in it. Press `tab`, or a view, to move the focus into this one.

<Canvas of={RailStories.Tv} />

`open` keeps a TV's rail open, or closed with `false`, whatever the focus.

## implementation notes

- It renders a `<nav>` named by `label`: “views” by default, in the page's language (`words.navigation.views`). Inside it, the views are a list, and the items of `head` and `foot` are lists of their own.
- Each item is an `<a href>`. Its `name` is visually hidden text inside the link and the link's `title`, the browser's own tooltip. Its icon is wrapped in an `aria-hidden` element.
- `label` on an item becomes the link's `aria-label`, for a name that says more: “notifications, 2 unread”. `count` draws the number on the icon, written in the page's language, and hidden from assistive technology.
- The current item's link has `aria-current="page"`; with `inside`, `aria-current="true"`. `current` is compared with each item's `href`, the items of `head` and `foot` included.
- `onNavigate` is called on a press of the main button without a modifier key, with the event and the link's `href`. A press that opens the link elsewhere doesn't call it. It is called for the current view too: go back to the top of the view there.
- `mark` renders a link before the lists, a tab stop of its own: the chevron, the first letter of `product`, and its name, `{product} home` (`words.navigation.home`), as hidden text and `title`. A press on it calls `onNavigate` with its `href`.
- The mark's cell is 56px high. Set `--nc-rail-head` on the rail to the height of the top bar beside it.
- One link has `tabindex="0"`: the current item's, or the first view's when none is current. The others have `-1`. While the focus is in the rail, the stop follows it; when the focus leaves, the stop is back on the current item.
- The rail is 80px wide and fills the height of what holds it; place it in the first column of the screen's grid, beside the top bar and the page. It adds `env(safe-area-inset-left)` and `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` to its padding, so it needs `viewport-fit=cover` in the page's viewport to keep clear of a phone's cutout and its own bar.
- A rail too low for its items scrolls.
- `data-nc-screen` on any ancestor chooses the look: none for a wide screen, `touch` for a phone held sideways, `tv` for a TV. The rail has no media queries of its own, except for reduced motion.
- On a TV, the `<nav>` keeps the width of the closed rail, 112px, and the open rail lies over the page beside it, as wide as its longest name and at least 320px. On a TV the `<nav>` has no height of its own: it takes the height of the row that holds it, so give that row the screen's height.
- On a TV, the rail is open while the focus is inside it: the `<nav>` has the class `nc-rail--open`. `open` sets it yourself.
- On a TV, when the focus moves into the rail from outside by anything but a press, it lands on the current item, whichever link was focused. Focus any link of the rail from the page's own arrow-key handling.
- On a TV, `→`, `esc` and a remote's back key (`GoBack`, `BrowserBack`) put the focus back on the element that had it before it entered the rail, and call `onClose`. If that element is gone, the focus stays in the rail: move it in `onClose`.
- After `onNavigate` on a TV, put the focus on the page you opened. The rail closes when the focus leaves it.
- An item with `keepCase` shows its name on a TV in the body face, with its own capitals: the person's name.
- More than five `views` logs a warning in the console.
- Other `<nav>` attributes and the `ref` reach the `<nav>`.
- axe-core finds no violations on a wide screen, on a touch screen, and on a TV, open and closed.

| key | does |
| --- | --- |
| `tab` | Moves onto the mark, then onto the current item (or the first view), then out of the rail. |
| `↓` / `↑` | Moves to the next / previous item, settings included. Past the last, it goes on at the first; on a TV it stops at the ends. |
| `home` / `end` | Moves to the first / last item. |
| `enter` | Opens the focused item, as on any link. |
| `→`, `esc`, back | On a TV: closes the rail and puts the focus back on the page. |
