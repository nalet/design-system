# app navigation

how a person moves between the views of one app: a side navigation that lists them, grouped by what people do there, with the view where the app opens at the top.

<Mock planned="SideNavigation, an app layout" label="a downloads app: the shell bar, the side navigation and the content">
  <AppLayoutMock />
</Mock>

the side navigation and the app layout that holds it are **planned** components. the mock-ups on this page are built from the components that exist, to show the proposal.

## when to use side navigation

- an app with more than four or five views, or with views of different kinds: daily work, finding things, setup.
- a vertical list scales, can be grouped, shows a count next to each view (`needs attention 3`), and stays visible while the content scrolls.

## when to use tabs instead

- **two to five facets of one object** — the sections of an object page: overview, episodes, files, history. use <C slug="tabs" />.
- **filters of one list** — <C slug="filter-tabs" /> with counts, in a [worklist](/design-system/patterns/worklist/).
- tabs switch what you look at; side navigation switches what you are doing.

## anatomy

<Mock planned="SideNavigation" label="the side navigation of a downloads app">
  <SideNavMock />
</Mock>

1. **groups**, in this order: **work** (the views people use daily, the exceptions view first), **find** (search, discover), **setup** (sources, clients, settings — only for people who may change them, at the bottom).
2. **each item** — an icon, a label, and an optional count: a number someone acts on, neutral unless it means attention.
3. **the current item** — marked by the accent bar on its leading edge and its weight, not by colour alone.

## behaviour

- **the first item is where the app opens**: its exceptions (see [principle 1](/design-system/foundations/principles/#1-the-system-does-the-work-people-handle-the-exceptions)), not its longest list.
- **setup is not daily work**: it sits in the setup group at the bottom of the app's navigation, and the shell's settings lists each app's setup state and links to those forms — see [settings and setup](/design-system/patterns/settings-and-setup/).
- **a view's address changes with the item**, so a tile, a link or the back button can land on it.
- **at medium widths** the navigation collapses to its icons.
- **on a phone** it becomes a menu behind the app's title, not a row of tabs that scrolls sideways.

## example: a downloads app

| group | items |
| --- | --- |
| work | `needs attention 3`, `on its way 12`, `requests`, `missing`, `series` |
| find | `search`, `discover` |
| setup | `search sources`, `clients`, `settings` |

## content

- **group titles** — `work`, `find`, `setup`: the same three words in every app.
- **items** — the view's noun, as people say it: `needs attention`, `on its way`, `search sources`. the same words as the view's title.
- **counts** — the number someone acts on, with its thousands separator. leave a count out when nobody acts on it.
