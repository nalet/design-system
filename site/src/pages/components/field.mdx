# field

A field puts a label, a hint and an error around one control, one under the other. It connects them to the control, so <Term id="assistive-technology">assistive technology</Term> reads them together.

<Example label="a field with a label and a hint">
  <Playground />
</Example>

## when to use

- Around every <C slug="input" />, <C slug="select" /> and <C slug="textarea" /> in a form. They have no label of their own, so the field gives them one.
- Around a <C slug="checkbox" /> or a <C slug="switch" />, only to add a hint or an error. Their own label already names them.
- Whenever a value can be wrong. The field shows the error in place of the hint, and marks the control as invalid.

## when to use something else

- **To label a group** of checkboxes, switches or radio buttons. A field labels one control. Put the group in the browser's own group frame, with a title for the whole group; [forms and settings](/design-system/patterns/forms-and-settings/) shows how.
- **Where a visible label doesn't fit**, such as a search box in a toolbar. Use an <C slug="input" /> with a search icon in front and a name that is read out but not shown, or use the <C slug="filter-bar" />.
- **For a message about the whole form**, such as “Could not save”. Use a <C slug="message-strip" /> above the form, as [message handling](/design-system/patterns/message-handling/) explains.

## anatomy

1. **Column.** The parts, one under the other, with a little space between them.
2. **Label** (optional). What the value is, in the <Term id="body-face">body face</Term>. It is shown just as it is written, in <Term id="sentence-case">sentence case</Term>.
3. **Required marker** (optional). A blue asterisk after the label. Assistive technology doesn't see it.
4. **Control.** The one control the field labels: an input, a select, a textarea, a checkbox or a switch.
5. **Message** (optional). The hint, in a dim colour, or the error, in red, both in small body text. The error takes the hint's place.

## variants and states

A field can carry a hint, be marked as required, or show an error. The error takes the hint's place, and it turns the border of an input, a select or a textarea red.

The same parts work around every control. Around a checkbox, leave the field's label out and keep the hint: the checkbox already names itself.

## behaviour

- The field adds no keys and no <Term id="tab-stop">tab stop</Term> of its own. <Kbd>tab</Kbd> goes straight to the control, and the control keeps its own keys.
- A click on the label puts <Term id="focus">focus</Term> in the control, or turns a checkbox or a switch on or off.

The full keyboard map is on the field's [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-inputs-field--docs).

## accessibility

- The label's text is the control's <Term id="accessible-name">accessible name</Term>. Nothing changes its letters, so write the label exactly as it should be read, in sentence case.
- A <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> reads the hint or the error after the control's name and its value.
- With an error, the control is marked as invalid for assistive technology too, not only by its red border.
- Around a checkbox or a switch, leave the field's label out. Two labels would name the control twice.

Today:

- The asterisk is only drawn, and assistive technology doesn't see it: the field doesn't make the control required. Make the control required itself, and say once per form what the asterisk means.
- An error isn't announced when it appears. When people send a form that has errors, move focus to the first invalid control, so its error is read out; see [message handling](/design-system/patterns/message-handling/).
- An error around a checkbox or a switch doesn't mark the box. It keeps its normal look, and the error text is the only sign.

## content

- **Label.** A noun phrase for the value, in sentence case, with no colon: “Release name”, “Region”, “Timeout (ms)”.
- **Hint.** What is accepted and why, in one line: “Lowercase letters, digits and hyphens”. Don't repeat the label.
- **Error.** What is wrong and how to fix it: “Use 63 characters or fewer”. No blame, no codes, and no “Invalid input”. The error takes the hint's place, so repeat the rule when people still need it.
- **Required.** Mark the required fields, and explain the asterisk once, at the top of the form. When most fields are required, mark the optional ones in their label instead: “Description (optional)”.

## specs

| part | in code | spec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Column | `Field` | A vertical stack, 8px (`--s-2`) between the parts. |
| Label | `label` | Inter 500, 13px, line height 1.4, `--fg-2`, no `text-transform`. A native `<label>` whose `for` points at the control. |
| Required marker | `required` | ` *` after the label, in `--cloud-blue`, `aria-hidden="true"`. It doesn't make the control required: pass `required` to the control as well. |
| Control | the one child element | An `Input`, `Select`, `Textarea`, `Checkbox` or `Switch`. |
| Hint | `hint` | Inter 12px, line height 1.4, `--fg-dim`. |
| Error | `error` | Inter 12px, line height 1.4, `--state-error`, in place of the hint. It sets `aria-invalid` and `invalid` on the control, so an input, a select or a textarea draws a `--state-error` border. |

- **Wiring:** the control's `id` is `htmlFor` when you pass it, else the child's own `id`, else a generated one. The label points at it, and the hint or the error joins the control's `aria-describedby`, so it is read after the name and the value.
- **Announcing:** the message is not a live region, so an error that appears isn't announced.
- **Groups:** a group of checkboxes, switches or radios is a native `<fieldset>` with a `<legend>`, not a field.
- **No room for a label:** an `Input` with an `aria-label` and a leading search icon.
- **Keyboard:** no keys and no tab stop of its own.
