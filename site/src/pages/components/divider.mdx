# divider

A divider is a thin line that separates two groups of content, where space alone doesn't make the break clear.

<Example label="a line between two groups">
  <Horizontal />
</Example>

## when to use

- To separate two groups of content in the same container, when space alone doesn't make the break clear.
- Vertically, between groups of short items in a row, such as counts, sizes and a timestamp.
- With a label on the line, for a quiet break in a long list: “older”, “archived”.

## when to use something else

- **To frame a region or mark its edge.** Use the border of a <C slug="box" /> or a <C slug="card" />. A divider is a break between things, not a box around them.
- **For a section title.** Use a <C slug="heading" />. A label on the line isn't read as a heading, so people can't find it or jump to it (see [accessibility](#accessibility)).
- **Between every item of a list.** Space already separates the items: use the gap of a <C slug="stack" />. A <C slug="table" /> draws the lines between its rows itself.

## anatomy

1. **Line.** A <Term id="hairline">hairline</Term> in a faint colour, or in a slightly stronger one for the heavier divider. A horizontal line runs the full width. A vertical line stretches to the height of its row, with a small minimum height.
2. **Label** (optional, horizontal lines only). A word or two in the middle of the line, with a little space on each side. It is small, slightly spaced-out <Term id="monospace">monospace</Term>, dim and lowercase, and it never wraps onto a second line.

A divider brings no space of its own. The space around it comes from the gap of the stack or the row it sits in.

## variants and states

The heavier divider is a little stronger than the standard one, and just as thin.

## behaviour

- A divider does nothing when it is clicked, and the keyboard passes over it: <Kbd>tab</Kbd> skips it.

The keyboard map is on the divider's [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-layout-structure-divider--docs).

## accessibility

- A <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> announces a divider as a separator.
- A divider that only decorates, inside something whose structure is already clear, can be hidden from <Term id="assistive-technology">assistive technology</Term>, so it isn't announced.
- The line is a faint decoration on purpose, with a very low <Term id="contrast">contrast</Term> against the background (see [shape](/design-system/styles/shape/#lines)). So a divider must never be the only thing that tells two groups apart: space and headings carry the structure.

Today: most screen readers don't read a divider's label. The label sits inside the separator, and a separator's contents aren't read. If the words matter, give the divider the same words as its <Term id="accessible-name">accessible name</Term>, or better, use a heading.

## content

- A label is one or two lowercase words that name what follows: “older”, “archived”, “2023”.
- No punctuation, no sentences, and nothing people need to act on.

## specs

| part | in code | spec |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Line | `Divider`, `orientation="horizontal"` (the default) | 1px of `--border`, the full width. |
| Heavier line | `heavy` | 1px of `--border-2`: a little stronger, still 1px. |
| Vertical line | `orientation="vertical"` | 1px wide, stretched to the height of its flex row (`align-self: stretch`), at least 16px (`--s-4`) tall. It ignores `label`. |
| Label | `label` | Horizontal only. Centred between two lines, 12px (`--s-3`) from each. JetBrains Mono 12px, letter-spacing 0.04em, `--fg-dim`, lowercased with `text-transform`, `white-space: nowrap`. |

- **Spacing:** no margin. The space around it comes from its parent's gap.
- **Role:** `role="separator"`; the unlabelled divider also sets `aria-orientation`. The label sits inside the separator and sets no `aria-label`: when the words matter, pass the same text as `aria-label`.
- **Decoration only:** `aria-hidden="true"` keeps a divider from being announced.
- **Contrast:** about 1.1:1 to 1.5:1 against the canvases, by design: the hairlines are decoration (see [shape](/design-system/styles/shape/#lines)).
- **Keyboard:** no focus and no action; not in the tab order.
