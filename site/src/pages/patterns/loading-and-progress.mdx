# loading and progress

Some things take a moment: a list loads, a file copies, a check runs. While people wait, the screen should tell them two things: that something is happening, and, when it can, how far along it is. It should do that quietly, in the place where the result will appear.

## which one

| the wait | show |
| --- | --- |
| Under about 0.4 seconds | Nothing: a loader that flashes looks like a glitch |
| Progress that is known, like a copy or an upload | A progress bar, with the value in words: “62% · 1.4 of 2.2 gb” |
| Unknown, in a button, a field or a toolbar | A small loader; the label stays |
| Unknown, in a panel or a player | A larger loader, with one line of text |
| A list or a page | Grey blocks in the shape of the content, not a loader in empty space |
| With <Term id="reduced-motion">reduced motion</Term> | The loader stands still, and the text says what is happening |
| For <Term id="screen-reader">screen readers</Term> | One status with a label, read out once |

## the loader

Our loader is a small square: a faint frame with three quarters of it lit in blue, and the gap running round it, one lap in four seconds. Every frame in the system is square; only the moving gap says “working”.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <LoaderSizes />
  <figcaption>The loader at three sizes. The unlit side is the gap, which runs round the frame.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **In a button, a field or a toolbar**, small, before the label, which stays. Inside a button it takes the label's colour, so it also shows on a blue button.
- **In a panel or a player**, larger, centred, with one line under it that says what is loading.
- **With reduced motion** it stands still, and its label, or the text beside it, says that something is loading.

The <C slug="spinner" /> page shows the loader live.

## wait a moment first

Most answers come quickly. If the loader appeared at once, it would flash for a split second on almost every click, and that reads as a glitch. So a load that finishes within about 0.4 seconds shows nothing new; the loader appears only after that.

## known progress

When the work knows how far it has come, show a bar and the value in words: the percentage and the amount, like “62% · 1.4 of 2.2 gb”. People read the words; the bar shows how far, at a glance. The progress bar is a planned component.

## a list or a page

A list or a page that loads for the first time shows grey blocks in the shape of what is coming: rows as long as its rows will be, a title where its title will be. These are <Term id="skeleton">skeleton</Term> blocks. The page doesn't jump when the content arrives, and people can already see what kind of thing is on its way.

Skeleton blocks are planned. Until they arrive, a first load shows the table's running line over an empty body.

A list that refreshes keeps the rows it has, at full strength, with a thin line running along its top edge. The <C slug="data-table" /> does this by itself.

## how it behaves

- **Show it where the content will be.** A busy button keeps its place and its label; a busy list keeps its frame and its rows.
- **One loader per region.** A page that loads three panels shows three loaders, one in each panel, rather than one over the whole page.
- **Stop the moment it is done**, and put the content in the loader's place, or what went wrong; see [empty states](/design-system/patterns/empty-states/) and [message handling](/design-system/patterns/message-handling/).

Today the components show their busy state the moment it is set; waiting 0.4 seconds first is up to the app.

## accessibility

- **One status, with a label, read out once**: “Loading the episode list”. Not a stream of updates, and not every few percent of a progress bar.
- **The area that is loading says so too.** A busy list or button is marked as busy for screen readers.
- **Motion never carries the message alone.** With reduced motion the loader stands still, and the text says what is happening.

Today the loader is read out as “loading”, and a busy button and a busy data table mark themselves as busy.

## the words

- **The line of text.** What is loading, in the item's own words, in sentence case: “Loading the episode list”, “Asking the catalog”. No “please wait”, and no “almost there”.
- **The value.** The percentage, then the amount: “62% · 1.4 of 2.2 gb”, with thousands separators and the unit.
- **When it takes long.** Say what is happening, not that it is slow: “Scanning 2,400 of 9,000 files”.
