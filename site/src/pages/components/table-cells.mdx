# table cells

three cells for a <C slug="data-table" />'s columns, so every table says the same things the same way: the status cell, a status and its reason; the date cell, a date absolute and relative; the identifier cell, what names the row.

<Example column label="the three cells in a data table">
  <CellsInATable />
</Example>

## when to use

- **status cell** — the state column of a data table: one status per row, from the object's one table of states, with the reason or the next step under it when there is one.
- **date cell** — a date column where age matters: aired, last searched, created, expires.
- **identifier cell** — the row header column: the id people know the row by, with a quieter second line that says more.

## when not to use

- **for a status outside a table** — beside a heading, in a card, in a sentence. use an <C slug="object-status" /> on its own; the status cell only adds the detail line, sized for a row.
- **for a message about the whole list** ("105 titles can't be checked"). that's a <C slug="message-strip" /> above the table, not a status in every row.
- **for tags, kinds and categories.** they aren't states. use a <C slug="badge" />.
- **for numbers and sizes.** a plain right-aligned column with the unit in the header — see the <C slug="table" />'s content rules.

## anatomy

### status cell

1. **wrapper** — a column with 3px between its lines, its items at the cell's leading edge, never wider than the cell.
2. **status** — an <C slug="object-status" />: a 20px chip with 8px side padding, a 1px `--state-*-border`, a `--state-*-bg` fill and `--state-*` text, the state's 12px glyph, jetbrains mono 11px, lowercase, on one line.
3. **detail** (optional) — jetbrains mono 11px, line-height 1.45, in `--fg-dim`, at most 44 characters wide. at most two lines show. it breaks anywhere, so a long id can't widen the column.

### date cell

1. **wrapper** — the parts on one baseline, 8px apart. in a narrow column the relative part wraps under the date. jetbrains mono 12px with tabular figures, so dates line up down a column.
2. **absolute date** — in `--fg-2`, lowercase: "12 oct 2026" (en-GB: day, short month, year). hovering it shows the full weekday, date and time.
3. **relative part** (`relative`, on by default) — in `--fg-dim`: "5d ago" on screen, "5 days ago" for a screen reader.
4. **empty** — an em dash in `--fg-dim`, read "no date".

### identifier cell

1. **wrapper** — a column, 2px between its lines.
2. **title** — jetbrains mono 13px in `--fg`: "S04E07", "worker-2".
3. **text** (optional) — inter 12px in `--fg-muted`, on one line. it ends in an ellipsis when its box is narrower than the text.

## variants and states

### status cell

the state sets the colour, the glyph and what a screen reader hears first; there is no colour prop. the five states, as the [status vocabulary](/design-system/patterns/status-vocabulary/) uses them:

| state | means | for instance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `neutral` | a plain fact | `missing` |
| `information` | under way, or worth knowing | `on its way` |
| `success` | done and good — never for a problem | `held` |
| `warning` | works, but someone should look | `can't be checked` |
| `error` | failed or blocked; needs action | `needs attention` |

the detail is the line under the status: why, or what happens next. the status can take object status's `text` appearance too, which drops the chip for dense rows, keeping the glyph and 12px text.

### date cell

the relative part counts whole calendar days, in the viewer's time zone:

| how far | on screen | read as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the same day | `today` | today |
| one day | `yesterday`, `tomorrow` | yesterday, tomorrow |
| 2 to 13 days | `5d ago`, `in 3d` | 5 days ago, in 3 days |
| 14 to 59 days | `7w ago` | 7 weeks ago |
| 60 days to just under two years | `20mo ago` | 20 months ago |
| two years or more | `2y ago` | 2 years ago |

### identifier cell

the title is what identifies the row; the text is a second line, when there is one to give. the title can be a link to the row's own page.

## behaviour

- the cells are text. none of them takes focus or handles a key; the keys belong to the row they sit in — see the <C slug="data-table" />. `tab` passes them by, unless an identifier's title is a link.
- the date cell works out "today" when it renders. it doesn't tick: a page left open past midnight says "today" about yesterday until it renders again.
- the hover texts — a status detail in full, a date's full timestamp — are the browser's own tooltips.

the keyboard map and the attributes the cells set are on their [react page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-data-display-table-cells--docs).

## accessibility

- **status cell** — the glyph is hidden from assistive tech: the text carries the state, and warnings and errors are read with their state first ("error: needs attention"). the detail is there in full for a screen reader, so it reads all of it, the lines cut off on screen included.
- **date cell** — the date is read as written ("12 Oct 2026"; the lowercase is css), then the relative part in words ("5 days ago"); the compact "5d ago" is hidden from screen readers. an empty value is read "no date".
- **identifier cell** — plain text. as the row header, it names the row for every other cell in it.

today:

- a status detail longer than two lines and a date's full timestamp are shown only in a native tooltip: on hover, with a mouse. keyboard and touch users can't open it. screen readers read the whole detail, but the time of day is only in that tooltip, which most of them don't read for plain text.
- the spoken words are fixed english: "no date", and the relative words ("5 days ago") whatever the language of the dates.
- an identifier's second line that doesn't fit is cut with an ellipsis and has no tooltip.

## content

- **status** — the state in the object's words, one to three words: `missing`, `on its way`, `needs attention`. write the vocabulary once, as the [status vocabulary](/design-system/patterns/status-vocabulary/) says, and use it in every row.
- **detail** — why, or what happens next, on one short line: `nothing found · again in 6h`, `waiting for the catalog`. a human reason, not a raw error or a code.
- **dates** — pass the value and let the cell format it; don't pre-format a string. keep `relative` where recency matters (aired, last searched); turn it off where it doesn't, like a fixed release date.
- **identifier** — the title is the id people use, `S04E07` or `worker-2`; the text is the human name, `the long way round`. never an internal uuid as the title.
