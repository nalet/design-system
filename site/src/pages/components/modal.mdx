# modal

A modal is a dialog that opens over the page for one task that needs an answer before the page can go on, such as confirming a delete. Until people answer or close it, the page behind it is dimmed and waits.

<Example label="confirm a delete, live">
  <Confirm client:visible />
</Example>

## when to use

- To confirm an action that deletes something or can't be undone, such as “delete volume” or “revoke token”.
- To collect two or three values for one task without leaving the page, such as “add server”.
- When the next step depends on the answer, and nothing else on the page should happen until it is given.

## when to use something else

- **To report that something happened.** Use a <C slug="toast" /> for a passing confirmation, or a <C slug="banner" /> for a message that stays on the page. A dialog that only says “saved” interrupts people and asks them nothing.
- **In front of an action that can be undone.** Do it, and confirm it with a toast. The way back is the opposite action on the page, because toasts carry no actions.
- **For a long form, or several steps.** Give it a page of its own; see [forms and settings](/design-system/patterns/forms-and-settings/).
- **For a short explanation.** An optional detail fits a <C slug="tooltip" />, and help for an input is the hint in its <C slug="field" />.
- **To offer a list of actions.** Use an <C slug="action-menu" />.

## anatomy

1. **Backdrop.** A dark veil over the whole page, in the colour of the <Term id="canvas">canvas</Term>, through which the page shows faintly.
2. **Dialog.** A square panel with a thin border, in the middle of the screen. It has a set maximum width, is never taller than the screen, and keeps a small margin from the screen's edges. A dialog that asks for it sits on the foot of a narrow screen instead, as [a sheet](#a-sheet-on-a-phone).
3. **Header** (only with a title). A thin rule runs below it. It holds:
   - **Title.** A heading in <Term id="monospace">monospace</Term>, shown in lowercase.
   - **Close button.** A small square with a cross, named “close” in the page's language.
4. **Body.** The content, in the <Term id="body-face">body face</Term>. When it is taller than the dialog, it scrolls, and the header and the footer stay in place.
5. **Footer** (optional). The actions, lined up on the right, with a thin rule above them. Where they don't fit side by side, as on a narrow screen with large text, each takes a line.

## variants and states

A modal can be set up in five ways. The example at the top of this page is a confirm.

| setup | how | use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confirm | A title, one or two sentences, and a footer with “cancel” and the action | An action that deletes something or can't be undone |
| Form | A title, fields in the body, and a footer | A few values for one task |
| No title | A name for <Term id="assistive-technology">assistive technology</Term> instead of a title | A short readout. With no header there is no close button, so put one in the footer |
| Persistent | <Kbd>esc</Kbd> and the backdrop don't close it | Work that a stray key shouldn't hide. The close button in the header and the buttons in the footer still close it |
| Sheet | The dialog asks to sit on the foot of a narrow screen | A short task or a set of choices on a phone, as [a sheet on a phone](#a-sheet-on-a-phone) describes. Never a confirm |

## a sheet on a phone

On a phone, a dialog for a short task or a set of choices can sit on the foot of the screen, as a <Term id="sheet">sheet</Term>. There the thumb reaches its buttons, and people know sheets from their phones. A dialog has to ask for it: one that doesn't sits in the middle on every screen, as the rest of this page describes.

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <SheetOnAPhone />
  <figcaption>The same dialog, which asks for a sheet: in a wide window it sits in the middle; on a phone it sits on the foot of the screen, with the dimmed page above it.</figcaption>
</figure>

This dialog asks for a sheet, and is 420 pixels wide at the most. Open it on a phone, or in a window narrower than 452 pixels, and it sits on the foot of the screen. In a wider window it sits in the middle.

<Example label="a dialog that is a sheet in a narrow window, live">
  <Sheet client:visible />
</Example>

### when a sheet fits

- **A short task or a set of choices.** Picking the lists for a title, or two or three values for one task: something people do with a thumb and are done with.
- **Never a question.** A confirmation before something that deletes or can't be undone stays in the middle, on a phone too: people read it before they reach its buttons.
- **Not for what a dialog is too small for.** A list of actions goes into an <C slug="action-menu" />, and a long form or a long list into a page of its own, on a phone as on a computer.

### when a dialog stays in the middle

- **When it doesn't ask.** Without being asked, no dialog becomes a sheet, however narrow the screen.
- **When there is room.** The modal measures its own room, the window, as the filter bar and the toolbar measure theirs; it doesn't ask what kind of device it is on. A dialog that asks is a sheet only where the window is narrower than the dialog's largest width and its two margins: 480 + 2 × 16 = 512px for a dialog of the usual width. So a narrow window on a computer, or a page zoomed to 400%, gets the sheet too, and a tablet with room for the dialog doesn't.
- **With larger text, sooner.** The dialog's width and its margins grow with the reader's text size, so the same window is too narrow for it sooner.

### how it behaves

- **Across the whole width**, on the foot of the screen, square like every frame. It is never taller than the screen less 48px, the room a dialog always leaves, so the dimmed page shows above it, and a press there closes it, as the backdrop does.
- **The same dialog.** The same header with its title and close button, the same body that scrolls, and the same footer, which stays above the strip a phone keeps at its foot for its own gestures.
- **At once.** It appears and goes without moving, as every dialog does: in this system panels don't slide ([motion](/design-system/styles/motion/#what-doesnt-move)).
- **The same ways out.** The close button, “cancel”, <Kbd>esc</Kbd> on a keyboard and the backdrop. The phone's own back closes it too, once the app passes it on to the dialog. There is no swipe down: nothing here depends on a gesture ([interaction](/design-system/foundations/interaction/#pointer-and-touch)).
- **The same focus.** <Term id="focus">Focus</Term> moves into it, stays inside it, and goes back to what opened it.

## behaviour

- **When it opens,** focus moves to the first control in the dialog. With a title, that is the close button in the header. Without one, it is the first control in the body or the footer, or the dialog itself when there is none.
- **While it is open,** <Kbd>tab</Kbd> and <Kbd>shift</Kbd> + <Kbd>tab</Kbd> stay inside the dialog: from its last control, focus wraps round to the first, and back. The page behind doesn't scroll.
- **Closing.** <Kbd>esc</Kbd>, a click on the backdrop and the close button all close it; a persistent modal ignores <Kbd>esc</Kbd> and the backdrop. When an <C slug="action-menu" /> in the dialog is open, the first <Kbd>esc</Kbd> closes the menu, and the next one closes the dialog.
- **When it closes,** focus goes back to whatever had it when the modal opened, usually the button that opened it.
- **On a TV,** the arrow keys of the remote move through the dialog's controls, in their order: down and right to the next one, up and left to the one before. They stop at the first and at the last, and never leave the dialog, the way <Kbd>tab</Kbd> stays inside it. A field keeps left and right for its own text.

The full keyboard map is on the modal's [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-overlays-modal--docs).

## accessibility

- **The title names the dialog.** Without a title, give the dialog an <Term id="accessible-name">accessible name</Term> instead. With neither, the dialog has no name.
- The title is always a level-2 heading.
- The close button is named “close”, in the page's language, and it is larger than the minimum <Term id="target-size">target size</Term>. On a screen made for touch it keeps its look, and answers to a touch in its <Term id="touch-area">touch area</Term>, 48 by 48 around it.
- A sheet is the same dialog to a <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term>: it has the same name, and nothing announces where it sits.
- A click on plain text in the dialog puts focus on the dialog itself; <Kbd>tab</Kbd> and <Kbd>shift</Kbd> + <Kbd>tab</Kbd> from there stay inside.

Today:

- You can't choose where focus starts. With a title, it always starts on the close button.
- A screen reader announces the title, not what will happen, so make the title say what happens: “delete volume”, not “are you sure?”.
- Open one modal at a time. Every open modal listens for <Kbd>esc</Kbd>, so one press closes them all.
- The page's scrolling is locked, but nothing makes up for the width of its scrollbar, so the page behind can shift sideways when the modal opens.
- If the control that opened the modal is gone when it closes, because you deleted its row for example, focus has nowhere to go back to. Move it yourself, to the next sensible place.

## content

- **Title.** A verb and its object, in the same words as the control that opened it: “delete volume”, “add server”. Not a question, and no full stop. The lowercase look is only a style, so write the title exactly as it should be read. A name in it keeps its capitals: “pick a release for The Quiet Orchard”.
- **Body.** One or two sentences, in <Term id="sentence-case">sentence case</Term>: what happens, to what, and whether it can be undone. Name the object: “This permanently deletes storage-01 and its 3 snapshots.”
- **Actions.** The button that confirms repeats the title's verb, as in “delete volume”, and the way out is “cancel”, as a quiet button. The confirming action goes last, on the right. Use the danger button only for an action that deletes something or can't be undone.

## specs

| part | spec |
| --- | --- |
| Layer | Nothing renders while `open` is `false`. Open, the modal renders into the page's `<body>` (a React portal): a fixed wrapper over the whole viewport, `role="presentation"`, `z-index: 100`, with 16px of padding, so the dialog keeps 16px from the viewport's edges. |
| Backdrop | `--bg`, the canvas colour, at 72% opacity, over the whole viewport. `aria-hidden="true"`. |
| Dialog | `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="true"`, `tabindex="-1"`. `--surface` fill, a 1px `--border-2` border, square, centred, text in `--fg-2`. As wide as there is room for, up to `width` (480px by default); at most the viewport's height minus 48px. `className` lands here. |
| Sheet | Only with `sheet`, and only where the window is narrower than `width` + 2 × 16px. The layer gets `nc-modal--sheet`: no padding, the dialog on its bottom edge and as wide as the window, at most the layer's height minus 48px (`--s-7`), with a 1px `--border-2` border on its top edge only. The dialog is padded by `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` at the foot, and by the left and right insets at the sides. Everything else is the dialog's. |
| Header | Only with `title`. Padding 12px 16px, 12px between the title and the close button, a 1px `--border` rule below. |
| Title | `title`: an `h2` that names the dialog (`aria-labelledby`). JetBrains Mono 600, 15px, `--fg`, lowercased with `text-transform`; a name wrapped in `KeepCase` keeps its case. |
| Close button | 26px square, above the 24px minimum target size, with a 14px × in `--fg-muted`; named “close” (`aria-label`), from the page's words: “schließen” on a German page. Hover: a `--surface-2` fill and `--fg`, in 120ms `ease`. Keyboard focus: the 2px `--focus-ring` outline, 1px outside. |
| Body | `children`. Padding 16px, Inter 14px, line-height 1.55. It scrolls (`overflow-y: auto`) when the content is taller than the dialog. |
| Footer | `footer`. The actions right-aligned, 8px apart, on a line each where they don't fit side by side; padding 12px 16px, a 1px `--border` rule above. |

| setting | in code | what it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open and close | `open`, `onClose` | <Kbd>esc</Kbd>, a click on the backdrop and the close button call `onClose`. The modal never closes itself: you set `open` to `false`. |
| No title | `ariaLabel` | Names the dialog (`aria-label`) when there is no `title`. |
| Persistent | `closeOnEsc={false}` and `closeOnBackdrop={false}` | Both are `true` by default. |
| Width | `width` | The largest width, in px at the usual text size. Default 480. |
| A sheet on a narrow screen | `sheet` | `false` by default. With it, the dialog is a sheet where the window is narrower than `width` + 32px, and sits in the middle everywhere else. |
| A form in the body | An `id` on the `<form>`, and `type="submit"` with `form={id}` on the footer button | The footer button sends the form, and so does <Kbd>enter</Kbd> in a field. |

- **Focus on open:** the first `button`, `[href]`, `input`, `select`, `textarea` or `[tabindex]` (other than `-1`) in the dialog, or else the dialog itself. On close, focus returns to the element that had it when the modal opened.
- **Focus trap:** <Kbd>tab</Kbd> and <Kbd>shift</Kbd> + <Kbd>tab</Kbd> wrap between the first and the last enabled controls; with none, <Kbd>tab</Kbd> does nothing.
- **Esc:** heard on the whole document, after the dialog's own controls, and only when none of them has called `preventDefault()` on it. That is how an open action menu takes the first press.
- **Scroll lock:** `overflow: hidden` on the page's `<body>` while open, with no room made for the scrollbar.
- **Motion:** none when it opens or closes, as a dialog or as a sheet.
- **The sheet's threshold:** measured, with no media query: a `ResizeObserver` on the backdrop, which covers the window, and one measurement before the first paint. The width counts in px at the usual text size, 16, so at twice the text size a window of 1,000px counts as 500. Where nothing can be measured (server rendering, jsdom tests), the dialog stays in the middle.
- **The strip at a phone's foot:** a browser reports the safe-area insets only on a page whose viewport meta tag has `viewport-fit=cover`; everywhere else they are 0.
- **The phone's back:** the app's to wire. When its history goes back while the dialog is open, it sets `open` to `false`.
- **On a touch screen** (`data-nc-screen="touch"`): the close button answers to a touch in 48 × 48px (`--s-7`), centred on it; the area stays inside the header, which is 50px high.
- **On a TV** (`data-nc-screen="tv"`): the layer's padding is the safe area, 27px at the top and the foot and 48px at the sides, and the dialog is at most the viewport's height minus 54px. The close button is 42px square; focused, it has a 3px `--focus-ring` outline 2px outside it, a `--surface-focus` fill and `--fg`, and grows by 1.05 in 150ms `ease-out`, without the growth under reduced motion. The arrow keys move through the first to the last enabled, visible control, in document order, and are stopped there; a text field, a text area, a select and a range keep left and right.
- **Where the attribute goes:** the modal renders into `<body>`, so its touch and TV rules apply when `data-nc-screen` is on `<html>` or `<body>`.
