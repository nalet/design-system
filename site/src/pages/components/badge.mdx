# badge

A small square label: mono, lowercase, hairline frame, 20px tall. Badge is for labels, counts and tags. The state of an object — running, failed, needs attention — is <C slug="object-status" />, not a badge.

<Example label="the four tones">
  <Tones />
</Example>

## when to use

- To tag an item with a short attribute that helps people scan and tell items apart: `gpu`, `read-only`, `4k`, `hdr`.
- For a count beside the thing it counts: `12 episodes`, `3 new`.
- For a short label that stays true for as long as the item exists: a version (`v2.4.1`), a `default` marker.

## when not to use

- **For the state of an object.** Use <C slug="object-status" />. It maps each state to a semantic colour and its own glyph, and announces warnings and errors in words. A badge's tone is only a colour: nothing stops a badge from saying `failed` in green.
- **For a message about the page or a task** — saved, could not load. Use a <C slug="message-strip" />, or a <C slug="toast" /> for something that just finished.
- **As a button or a filter chip.** A badge is static: it can't take focus or be pressed. Use a <C slug="button" />, or <C slug="filter-tabs" /> to filter a list.
- **For a count on a launchpad tile.** The <C slug="tile" /> has its own badge.

## anatomy

1. **Container** — inline, 20px tall, padding 0 8px, 1px border, square. `neutral` sits on `--surface-2` with a `--border-2` frame; a tone sits on a 12% tint of its colour over `--bg-2`, framed in a 45% mix of its colour and `--border-2`.
2. **Dot** (optional) — a 6px square in the label's colour, 8px before the text. Hidden from assistive tech.
3. **Label** — JetBrains Mono 11px, letter-spacing 0.02em, shown lowercase, never wraps.

## variants and states

Four tones. `neutral` is the default and the right choice for most tags; a tone is only a colour, so use one where it helps people find a label, and keep its meaning the same everywhere.

| tone | text | use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `neutral` | `--fg-muted` | Tags, counts, versions: the default |
| `blue` | `--cloud-blue` | Information worth spotting: `new`, `default` |
| `green` | `--signal-green` | A positive attribute: `verified`, `included` |
| `amber` | `--signal-amber` | An attribute that asks for care: `read-only`, `deprecated` |

There is no red tone. An error is a state: <C slug="object-status" /> for one object, a <C slug="message-strip" /> for the page.

The `dot` puts a 6px square in the label's colour before the text. It adds no meaning of its own — it repeats the tone — and it makes a badge look like a status light. Use it as a colour key, where the same colour appears nearby (a legend); if you reach for it to show state, you want object status.

## behaviour

- A badge is static: it has no focus, no keys and no action of its own.
- It never wraps and has no maximum width: a long label runs past its container rather than breaking.

The keyboard map, the element it renders and what passes through are on its [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-data-display-badge--docs).

## accessibility

- Screen readers read its text inline with what's around it.
- The tone is never announced, and neither is the dot. The words carry the meaning: `read-only` is read, the amber is not.
- The lowercase look is CSS, so screen readers read the text as you wrote it.
- A bare number is read without its subject ("12"). Keep a count right next to what it counts, or say it: `12 files`.
- Every tone's text meets 4.5:1 on its own tint; `green` is the closest, at about 4.9:1. The frame is decorative.

## content

- One or two words, lowercase, no full stop: `gpu`, `read-only`, `3 new`.
- Describe what the item is, not what it is doing right now. "running" is a state; "gpu" is a tag.
- Digits for counts: `12`, not `twelve`.
- Don't repeat the column or field name in every badge: `gpu`, not `label: gpu`.

## do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="Badges as tags: what the item is. Neutral unless a colour helps.">
    <Badge>gpu</Badge>
    <Badge>batch</Badge>
    <Badge>stateful</Badge>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="A state in a badge. The tone is only a colour, so nothing stops it saying failed in green. Use object status.">
    <Badge tone="green">failed</Badge>
    <Badge tone="amber">running</Badge>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="Neutral by default, one tone where it helps people find a label.">
    <Badge>v2.4.1</Badge>
    <Badge>12 files</Badge>
    <Badge tone="blue">new</Badge>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="A colour on every tag means nothing; people look for a pattern that isn't there.">
    <Badge tone="blue">gpu</Badge>
    <Badge tone="green">batch</Badge>
    <Badge tone="amber">http</Badge>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="A word or two. The explanation goes in text beside it.">
    <Badge tone="amber">read-only</Badge>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="A sentence in a badge never wraps; it runs out of its container.">
    <Badge tone="amber">read-only until the nightly backup has finished</Badge>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>
