# spinner

A faint, still square frame with three quarters of it lit, and the gap running round it, one lap in four seconds: the one moving thing in an otherwise still interface. It says "working on it" when you can't say how long.

## when to use

- For a short wait of unknown length in a small region: one value, a count, a panel's content.
- In place of a value while it loads, with a label that names the value.
- Beside a short line of text that says what is loading.

## when not to use

- **For an action the user started.** Use the busy state of the <C slug="button" /> (or <C slug="icon-button" />). The spinner sits inside the control that was pressed, and the button ignores a second press.
- **While a table's rows load.** Use the <C slug="data-table" />'s loading state: it marks the table busy and runs a progress rule along its top edge, so the wait shows where the rows will be.
- **For the result.** When the work ends, replace the spinner with what happened: the content, a <C slug="toast" /> for a background job that finished, a <C slug="message-strip" /> for a failure.
- **For long work with known progress.** Today there is no progress bar. Say the progress in <C slug="text" /> next to the spinner: `12 of 40 copied`.

## anatomy

1. **Frame** — a square the spinner's size, 16px by default, with square corners, in `--cloud-blue` unless it is given another colour. Drawn at 20% opacity, it is always there and never moves.
2. **Lit part** — three quarters of the frame in full colour, with square ends. It starts in the middle of the top edge, so no corner ever shows a notch.
3. **Gap** — the quarter that isn't lit. It runs round the frame; nothing else moves.

The stroke is 2px up to 24px, then a pixel more for every 16px: 3px at 40px.

## variants and states

The size is any number of pixels, and the stroke scales with it. Buttons use 12px (small) and 14px (medium, large); 14px also sits well beside 13px text; 16px on its own; 24 to 40px for a panel.

The frame is `--cloud-blue` by default — on its own it does not pick up the colour of the text around it. Inside a button it takes the label's colour, so it shows on a primary button's blue fill too. Anywhere else it can take any colour: a token such as `--fg-muted`, or the colour of the text around it.

## behaviour

- The frame stays still while the gap runs round it at a steady pace, one lap in four seconds, without end. It takes no focus.
- Under reduced motion it stands still, three quarters lit, and its label carries the state.
- It never stops by itself. Remove it when the work ends.

What to show while something loads, and when, is [loading and progress](/design-system/patterns/loading-and-progress/).

The keyboard map and the attributes it sets are on its [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-feedback-status-spinner--docs).

## accessibility

- A spinner is a status, named "loading" unless it is given a label.
- Give the label the specific thing: `loading volumes`, not the default.
- Beside visible text that says the same thing, the label is read twice. Hide the spinner from assistive tech and let the text speak.
- Under reduced motion nothing moves, so the words carry the state: the label, or the text beside the spinner.
- The lit part meets 3:1 against every background (`--cloud-blue` is at least 6.8:1); the faint frame is decoration. A custom colour must keep 3:1.

Today:

- The label is a name, not text content, and a live region announces changes to its text. A spinner added to the page carries no text, so most screen readers won't announce it appearing. When the wait matters, put a short message in a live region that is already on the page and update its text.

## content

- The label says what is loading, without an ellipsis: `loading volumes`, `checking storage-01`. It is read, not shown.
- Text beside it says the same words, in sentence case: `Loading volumes`. No "please wait".
- When a wait runs past a few seconds, say more: what is happening, and whether people can leave the page meanwhile.
