# stack

a flex column or row whose `gap` comes from the space scale. the workhorse for laying out everything else.

<Canvas of={StackStories.Playground} />

## when to use

- to lay out siblings in one direction with even space between them: a form's fields, a row of badges, a header with a title and an action.
- wherever you would write `display: flex` and a `gap`.

## when not to use

- **for a grid of items in rows and columns.** a stack runs in one direction. launchpad tiles go in a [tile group](?path=/docs/components-navigation-tile-group--docs); for other grids, use css grid.
- **for a row of actions.** use a [toolbar](?path=/docs/components-actions-toolbar--docs): it sets the emphasis and moves what doesn't fit into an overflow menu.
- **for padding or a background.** a stack only spaces its children. padding around them is a [box](?path=/docs/components-layout-structure-box--docs) or a [card](?path=/docs/components-layout-structure-card--docs).

## anatomy

1. **container** — a `div` with `display: flex` (`inline-flex` with `inline`), `box-sizing: border-box` and `min-width: 0`, so it can shrink inside other flex and grid layouts.
2. **gap** — between children only, never around them: 0, 4, 8, 12, 16 (default), 24, 32, 48 or 64px, from the `--s-*` tokens.
3. **children** — laid out in source order.

## variants and states

| prop | values | default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `direction` | `vertical` (a column), `horizontal` (a row) | `vertical` |
| `gap` | 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 | 16 |
| `align` | `start`, `center`, `end`, `stretch`, `baseline` — across the stack | not set: children stretch |
| `justify` | `start`, `center`, `end`, `between`, `around` — along the stack | not set: children pack at the start |
| `wrap` | children flow onto a new line when they run out of room | off |
| `inline` | renders `inline-flex`: the stack is as wide as its content and sits in the line of text | off |

without `align`, children stretch across the stack: full width in a vertical stack, full height in a row.

## behaviour and keyboard

| key | does |
| --- | --- |
| `tab` | skips the stack; focus moves through its children in source order. |

- there is no reverse direction, so what people see, what they read and where focus goes stay in the same order. keep it that way: don't reorder children with css `order` or a reversed `flex-direction`.

## accessibility

- a plain `div` with no role. it adds no structure for assistive tech.
- a stack of similar items is not a list to a screen reader. for a real list, render `ul` and `li`, or pass `role="list"` to the stack and `role="listitem"` to each child.
- today: it is always a `div`; there is no `as` prop.

## content

a stack has no text of its own. order its children the way people read and act: what the group is about first, its actions last.

## props

`Stack` passes every native `div` attribute through and forwards its ref. your `style` is merged over the base layout, so it can change `display`, the direction and the `gap`; `align`, `justify` and `wrap` are applied after it and win.
