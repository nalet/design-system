# message strip

A message strip tells people something about the content of the page, such as titles that can't be checked, in one or two plain sentences and with a way forward. The technical part waits behind “details”.

  <Playground client:visible />
</Example>

## when to use

- For a condition of the page's content that lasts until it is resolved or dismissed: “105 titles can't be checked: the catalog holds them without an identity.”
- For an error from loading or from an action, with a way forward: “Couldn't load the list.” and “try again”.
- Above the content it is about. Show one or two at a time at most: with more, none of them reads as important.

## when to use something else

- **To confirm an action someone just took.** For “8 episodes queued”, use a <C slug="toast" />: it says so and leaves.
- **For one item.** A field's error goes in the error text of its <C slug="field" />, and a row's state in an <C slug="object-status" /> in the row.
- **In place of a list that has nothing to show.** When the list is empty, or couldn't load and there is nothing older to show, use an <C slug="illustrated-message" /> where the list would be. Keep the strip for content that is still on the screen.
- **To stop everything for a decision.** Use a <C slug="modal" />.
- **For help that is always true about one control.** Put it in the hint of the control's <C slug="field" />.

## anatomy

1. **Strip.** A square box, tinted and framed in shades of the state's colour, with a thicker bar in the colour itself down its left edge.
2. **<Term id="glyph">Glyph</Term>.** The state's small symbol, in its colour, beside the first line of text. <Term id="assistive-technology">Assistive technology</Term> skips it.
3. **Text.** One or two sentences, in the <Term id="body-face">body face</Term>. It takes the free width.
4. **Actions** (optional). Small buttons after the text. When there isn't room beside it, they move under it.
5. **Details toggle** (when there are details). A quiet text button, “details”, with a small arrow that turns down while the details are open.
6. **Details** (optional). The technical part, under the text, in <Term id="monospace">monospace</Term>, set off by a thin line on its left. Line breaks are kept, and long ids break anywhere.
7. **Dismiss** (optional). A small square button with a cross, at the right edge.

## variants and states

There are five states: neutral, information, success, warning and error. Information is the default. Each has its own colour and its own glyph.

A <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> announces each state in its own way: politely, at its next pause, or at once, interrupting what it is reading.

| state | what a screen reader says | use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral | The text alone, politely | A plain fact about the content: what runs on its own, what is switched off |
| Information | “information:” and the text, politely | Under way, or worth knowing: the list is being refreshed, an index is rebuilding |
| Success | “success:” and the text, politely | A good condition that lasts, such as every source answered in the last hour. Or what a save changed on this page: “Saved. Times on this page now show in UTC.” A plain confirmation is a toast |
| Warning | “warning:” and the text, politely | It works, but someone should look: titles that can't be checked, sources over their limit, a refresh that failed while the last data still shows |
| Error | “error:” and the text, at once | Failed or blocked, and needs action: an action that failed, a list that couldn't load. Give it a way forward |

### details

The details keep the technical part, such as ids, raw errors and HTTP codes, behind a <Term id="disclosure">disclosure</Term>, in monospace. They stay closed until someone opens them, and an announcement never reads them out.

### dismiss

A strip can carry a dismiss button. Offer it for a message people can read and move on from, such as what a save changed, or an action that failed. Leave it off a condition that is still true, like a list that can't load.

The strip doesn't remember that it was dismissed: the screen does, and brings the strip back when its condition changes again.

## behaviour

- The strip's controls take <Term id="focus">focus</Term> in this order: its actions, then the details toggle, then dismiss. The text takes no focus.
- <Kbd>enter</Kbd> or <Kbd>space</Kbd> on the details toggle shows or hides the details.
- <Kbd>esc</Kbd> does nothing. A strip is part of the page; it doesn't float above it, as a dialog does.
- The strip announces its text when it appears or changes. [Accessibility](#accessibility), below, says what that means for each state.

The full keyboard map is on the message strip's [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-feedback-status-message-strip--docs).

## accessibility

- **Only the text is read out.** The text is a <Term id="live-region">live region</Term>. An update reads the whole message, and never the actions, the details or dismiss.
- **Errors interrupt.** An error is announced at once; every other state politely, when the screen reader has a pause. A strip that is on the page from the start and doesn't change can be a still note instead, which isn't announced at all: it is then read with the page, like any other text.
- **The state word.** Every state but neutral reads its name before the text, from words that are hidden on screen: “warning: 105 titles can't be checked”. The word can be translated, given to neutral as well, or left out. An <C slug="object-status" /> names only warning and error.
- **Not by colour alone.** The glyph is hidden from assistive technology, and each state has a glyph of its own shape, so the state never rests on colour alone.
- **Closed details** are neither read out nor reachable with the keyboard.
- **Dismiss** is named “dismiss”, and it is larger than the minimum <Term id="target-size">target size</Term>. The details toggle and dismiss show the same focus outline as every control; [interaction](/design-system/foundations/interaction/#focus) explains it.

Today:

- A live region announces changes to text it already holds. So a strip that is announced politely, and appears together with its text, may go unannounced in some screen readers. A strip that interrupts, as an error does, is announced as it appears.
- Dismissing removes the button that had focus, and focus falls back to the page. Move it yourself, to the content's heading or to the next control.
- The details toggle and dismiss have the same names on every strip. With two strips on a page, name them so that people can tell them apart.

## content

- **The text.** One or two plain sentences: what is true, then what it means or what happens next. “105 titles can't be checked: the catalog holds them without an identity.” It is in the body face, the reader's language, so it is written in <Term id="sentence-case">sentence case</Term>. No ids, codes or raw errors.
- **Numbers.** Exact, with a thousands separator: “2,500”, not “2.5k”.
- **An error** says what failed, and offers the way forward: “Couldn't load the list.” and “try again”. When the last data still shows, keep it, and make the strip a warning that says how old it is: “Couldn't refresh the list; it shows what was loaded 4m ago.”
- **Actions.** One or two small quiet buttons, each a verb, in the lowercase monospace voice: “show them”, “try again”, “ask the catalog again”. Not the emphasized button: the view's one emphasized action is elsewhere.
- **Details.** The raw part, as it came: “GET /api/missing: http 502 bad gateway”, “request id 7d1c0e4a”. Line breaks are kept.

## specs

| state | in code | announced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral | `state="neutral"` | Politely (`role="status"`), with no state word |
| Information | `state="information"` (the default) | Politely, after “information:” |
| Success | `state="success"` | Politely, after “success:” |
| Warning | `state="warning"` | Politely, after “warning:” |
| Error | `state="error"` | At once (`role="alert"`), after “error:” |

Each state sets three colours: `--state-<state>` for the bar on the left and the glyph, `--state-<state>-border` for the frame, the details rule and the dismiss button's frame on hover, and `--state-<state>-bg` for the fill. The strip carries `data-state`.

| part | spec |
| --- | --- |
| Strip | A row: the glyph, then the text with its actions and details, then dismiss. Padding 12px, 16px on the left. A 3px rule on the left in `--state-*`, a 1px `--state-*-border` frame, a `--state-*-bg` fill, square corners. Inter 13px, line-height 1.5, `--fg`. The glyph and the text 12px apart. |
| Glyph | The state's 14px glyph in `--state-*`, set 3px down so it sits on the first line. `aria-hidden`. `icon`: `true` (the default), `false` to hide it, or an element of your own. |
| Text | `children`. It takes the free width, at least 24ch before the actions wrap under it; 16px from the actions beside it, 4px above them once they wrap. |
| Actions | `actions`. 8px apart, wrapping under the text when there is no room. One or two buttons, `size="sm"` and `variant="ghost"`, never `variant="primary"`. |
| Details toggle | Shown when `details` is set, named by `detailsLabel` (default “details”). A quiet text button: JetBrains Mono 12px, `--fg-muted` (`--fg` on hover), lowercase, 28px tall, padding 0 8px. A 10px caret that turns 90° to point down while the details are open, in 120ms `ease`, with no movement under reduced motion. `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`. |
| Details | `details`; `defaultDetailsOpen` opens them on the first render. 8px under the text. JetBrains Mono 12px, line-height 1.55, `--fg-muted`, padding 8px 12px, a 1px `--state-*-border` rule on the left. Line breaks kept (`white-space: pre-wrap`), long IDs broken anywhere (`overflow-wrap: anywhere`). `hidden` while closed, and outside the live region. |
| Dismiss | Shown when `onDismiss` is set, named by `dismissLabel` (default “dismiss”). A 28px square at the right edge (above the 24px minimum target size), with a 12px × in `--fg-muted`; on hover, `--fg` and a `--state-*-border` frame. The strip doesn't hide itself: `onDismiss` is called, and you stop rendering it. |
| Focus | The details toggle and dismiss: the 2px `--focus-ring` outline, 1px outside, on keyboard focus only. |

| setting | in code | what it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Announcing | `live`: `'polite'`, `'assertive'` or `'off'` | Polite gives the text `role="status"`, assertive `role="alert"`, off no role at all: a still note, read with the page. The default is assertive for `error` and polite for the rest. The text is `aria-atomic`, so an update reads the whole message. |
| State word | `stateLabel` | Visually hidden text before the message: the state's name, for every state but `neutral`. A string replaces the word (and gives `neutral` one); `false` leaves it out. |
