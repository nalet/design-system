# stack

a column or a row whose gap comes from the space scale. the workhorse for laying out everything else.

<Example label="three items in a column, 16px apart">
  <Playground />
</Example>

## when to use

- to lay out siblings in one direction with even space between them: a form's fields, a row of badges, a header with a title and an action.
- wherever you would write `display: flex` and a `gap`.

## when not to use

- **for a grid of items in rows and columns.** a stack runs in one direction. launchpad tiles go in a <C slug="tile-group" />; for other grids, use css grid (see [layout](/design-system/foundations/layout/#grid-and-layout)).
- **for a row of actions.** use a <C slug="toolbar">toolbar</C>: it sets the emphasis and moves what doesn't fit into an overflow menu.
- **for padding or a background.** a stack only spaces its children. padding around them is a <C slug="box" /> or a <C slug="card" />.

## anatomy

1. **container** — a column or a row. it can shrink inside other layouts.
2. **gap** — between children only, never around them: a step of the [space scale](/design-system/foundations/layout/#the-scale), 16px by default.
3. **children** — laid out in source order.

## variants and states

| option | values | default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| direction | `vertical` (a column), `horizontal` (a row) | `vertical` |
| gap | a step of the scale, from 0 to 64px | 16px |
| align | `start`, `center`, `end`, `stretch`, `baseline` — across the stack | not set: children stretch |
| justify | `start`, `center`, `end`, `between`, `around` — along the stack | not set: children pack at the start |
| wrap | children flow onto a new line when they run out of room | off |
| inline | the stack is as wide as its content and sits in the line of text | off |

with no alignment set, children stretch across the stack: full width in a vertical stack, full height in a row.

## behaviour

- a stack takes no focus: `tab` moves through its children in source order.
- there is no reverse direction, so what people see, what they read and where focus goes stay in the same order. keep it that way: don't reorder children with css `order` or a reversed `flex-direction`.

the keyboard map and the element it renders are on its [react page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-layout-structure-stack--docs).

## accessibility

- a stack adds no structure for assistive tech: it has no role.
- a stack of similar items is not a list to a screen reader. when the items are a list, mark them up as one.

## content

a stack has no text of its own. order its children the way people read and act: what the group is about first, its actions last.

## do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="children in the order people read and tab: cancel, then the emphasized action.">
    <Stack direction="horizontal" gap={8}>
      <Button variant="ghost">cancel</Button>
      <Button variant="primary">save changes</Button>
    </Stack>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="the same look made with row-reverse. tab now reaches save before cancel, against what people see.">
    <Stack direction="horizontal" gap={8} style={{ flexDirection: 'row-reverse' }}>
      <Button variant="primary">save changes</Button>
      <Button variant="ghost">cancel</Button>
    </Stack>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>
