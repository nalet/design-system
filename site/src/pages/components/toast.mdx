# toast

A toast is a short message at the bottom of the screen that confirms an action someone just took, such as “8 episodes queued”. It never takes <Term id="focus">focus</Term>, it waits for a pause before a <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> reads it, and it leaves on its own.

  <Playground client:visible />
</Example>

## when to use

- To confirm an action the person just took, once it is done or under way: “8 episodes queued”, “Settings saved”, “Asking the catalog again — the list follows”.
- When the result shows on the page as well, such as rows that change to “on its way”, and the toast only says that it happened.
- After an action on several items from a <C slug="toolbar">selection toolbar</C>, with the count: “8 episodes queued · 3 skipped: held already or under way”.

## when to use something else

- **For an error that needs acting on.** Use an error <C slug="banner" /> with a way forward. An error has to stay until it is resolved, offer “try again”, and be announced at once. A toast does none of that: it leaves after five seconds, can't take focus, holds no button, and is read only when the screen reader has a pause.
- **For a condition that lasts**, such as “2 sources are over their daily limit”. Use a <C slug="banner" />.
- **To ask something, or to offer undo or a link.** A toast has nothing to press. When an action can't be undone, ask first, in a <C slug="modal" />.
- **For the state of one item.** That is a <C slug="status" /> in its row.
- **For anything people must not miss.** After five seconds it is gone, and nothing brings it back.

## anatomy

1. **Toaster.** The place where toasts appear: at the bottom of the screen, in the middle, above everything else on the page, an open modal or action menu included. A screen whose foot is taken moves it [to the top](#at-the-top). It is a region named “notifications”, in the page's language. It never reaches the edges of the screen, and clicks pass through it everywhere except on the toasts.
2. **List.** The toasts, stacked a small step apart and centred, with the newest at the bottom; at the top, the newest comes first.
3. **Toast.** A square box with a thin frame and a bar in the state's colour on its left edge. Its text is in the <Term id="body-face">body face</Term>, because a toast is read. It slides up a little as it fades in, or down where the toasts are at the top; with <Term id="reduced-motion">reduced motion</Term> it simply appears. It leaves without an animation.
4. **<Term id="glyph">Glyph</Term>.** The state's small symbol, in its colour. <Term id="assistive-technology">Assistive technology</Term> skips it.
5. **Message.** The text, in sentence case.

## variants and states

The state sets the glyph and the colour of the bar.

| state | use it for | for example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral (the default) | A plain fact about what happened | “3 skipped: held already or under way” |
| Information | Something started that finishes later | “Asking the catalog again — the list follows” |
| Success | Done | “8 episodes queued”, “Settings saved” |
| Warning | Done, with a part that didn't go through | “6 queued · 2 skipped: their source is over its daily limit” |

There is an error state too. Don't use it: an error belongs in a banner, as [when to use something else](#when-to-use-something-else) explains.

### at the top

Some screens keep their foot for something people use the whole time, such as a video's controls and the subtitles above them. There the toasts appear at the top, in the middle, and nothing else about them changes: the same toast, the same five seconds, three at most, read out the same way.

Open the player, save the title and add it to the film club. While the player is open, every toast on this page appears at the top, below the bar of this site, the newest first. Close the player, and the toasts are back at the bottom.

<Example label="a player that moves the toasts to the top while it is open, live">
  <AtTheTop client:visible offset={56} />
</Example>

- **The screen moves the toaster, not one toast.** A screen whose foot is taken, such as a player that fills the window, moves the toasts to the top while it is open, and back when it closes. In that time every toast shows at the top, so they never split between two places.
- **The newest nearest the edge.** At the top, the newest toast comes first and the older ones move down. It comes down a few pixels as it fades in, where at the bottom it rises.
- **Clear of the bars.** 24px below the top edge, as it is 24px above the bottom one. Where a bar stays at the top, the screen says how high it is, and the toasts appear 24px below the bar, so they never cover its controls. On a TV, the distance from the edge is the <Term id="safe-area">safe area</Term>'s 27 dp, at the top as at the bottom ([TV focus](/design-system/patterns/tv-focus/#movement-and-space)).
- **Above a bar at the foot.** Where only a bar takes the foot, such as a phone's tab bar, the toasts stay at the bottom, 24px above the bar. The app says once how high the bar is.
- **Clear of the phone's own strips.** A phone keeps a strip at the top and at the foot of its screen for itself. Where a page reaches under them, the toasts keep their distance from the strip, not from the glass.

## behaviour

- Nothing in a toast can take focus, so a toast has no keys: <Kbd>tab</Kbd> never reaches it, and <Kbd>esc</Kbd> does nothing. It leaves on its own.
- **Timing.** A toast leaves after five seconds, counted from when it shows. While the pointer rests on it, the count pauses; when the pointer moves off, the count goes on with the time that was left.
- **The queue.** A toast that replaces one already showing takes the newest place, with a fresh timer.

The full keyboard map is on the toast's [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-feedback-status-toast--docs).

## accessibility

- The toaster is a region named “notifications”, in the page's language. A screen reader reads each new toast once, after whatever it is saying. A toast that leaves is not announced.
- Where the toasts appear changes nothing for a screen reader: at the top they are read in the same order, and moving them is not announced.
- Nothing takes focus. A toast never moves the reader, never interrupts typing, and adds no <Term id="tab-stop">tab stop</Term>.
- The glyph is hidden from assistive technology. A warning or error toast says its state first, in the page's language, such as “warning: …”, like a <C slug="status" />. The calmer states read only the message, so put what matters in the words: “6 queued · 2 skipped”.
- With reduced motion, toasts appear without sliding in.

Today:

- Only the pointer can pause a toast. People who use a keyboard or a screen reader can't pause it or bring it back, so a toast must never be the only record of what happened: the page shows the result too (<Term id="wcag">WCAG</Term> 2.2.1).
- There is no way to dismiss a single toast by hand, with the pointer or with the keyboard. It leaves when its time is up.

## content

- **What just happened**, with the object and the count: “8 episodes queued”, “Settings saved”, “api-7f9c restarted”. Write it in sentence case, because the message is in the body face, the reader's language, and leave out the full stop. Use the past tense for what is done, and the -ing form for what is under way.
- **A part that didn't go through** follows a middle dot (·), with the reason: “8 episodes queued · 3 skipped: held already or under way”.
- **One line.** A toast leaves after five seconds, so it has to be read at a glance.
- **Text only.** A link or a button in a toast would vanish after five seconds, and the keyboard couldn't reach it.
- **Never an error that needs doing something about.** That is a <C slug="banner" />.

## specs

| part | spec |
| --- | --- |
| Toaster | `Toaster`: mount exactly one, near the app's root; every mounted toaster shows every toast. A `section` named by `label` (default “notifications”, from the page's words: “benachrichtigungen” on a German page), fixed at the bottom centre, 24px (`--s-5`) above the viewport's bottom edge, `z-index: 200` (above a modal at 100 and an action menu at 150), at most the viewport's width minus 32px. `pointer-events: none`, except on the toasts. |
| List | An `ol` that is a polite live region (`aria-live="polite"`, `aria-relevant="additions text"`), there before the first toast. Toasts 8px apart, centred, the newest last; at most three, and a fourth pushes out the oldest. |
| Toast | An `li`. `--surface-2` fill, a 1px `--border-2` frame, a 3px left rule in the state's colour, square corners, padding 8px 16px (12px on the left). Inter (`--ff-ui`) 13px / 1.4 in `--fg`. The glyph and the message 8px apart. |
| Glyph | The state's 12px glyph in its colour, `aria-hidden`. |
| Entrance | Slides up 6px and fades in over 160ms `ease-out`; no animation with reduced motion. No exit animation. |
| State in words | A visually hidden “warning: ” or “error: ” before the message of a warning or error toast, from the page's words: “warnung: ” and “fehler: ” on a German page. |
| At the top | Set by the screen while it is open, for every toast, or by the toaster's `placement`: fixed at the top centre, 24px below the viewport's top edge (`nc-toaster--top`, `data-placement="top"`). The list is drawn newest first (`column-reverse`; the order in the document stays), and the entrance slides down 6px. The rest is as at the bottom. |
| A bar at the edge | `offset`, the bar's height in px at the usual text size: the toasts keep their 24px from the bar. It is added to the distance from the edge, as `--nc-toaster-bar`, in rem. |
| The phone's strips | The distance from the edge grows by `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, or by `env(safe-area-inset-top)` at the top. A browser reports them only on a page whose viewport meta tag has `viewport-fit=cover`; everywhere else they are 0. |
| On a TV | With `data-nc-screen="tv"` on an element around the toaster: 27px from the top or the foot, the safe area, in place of 24px; at most the viewport's width minus 96px; the message at 14px. |

| setting | in code | what it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Show a toast | `toast(message, options)` | Shows it and returns its id. With no `Toaster` mounted, it warns in the console and nothing shows. |
| State | `state`: `'neutral'` (the default), `'information'`, `'success'` or `'warning'`. `'error'` exists, but errors belong in a banner | The glyph, and the rule in `--state-neutral`, `--state-information`, `--state-success` or `--state-warning`. |
| Time on screen | `duration`: 5000 (ms) by default | Counted from when the toast shows; hover pauses it. |
| Replace | `id` | A showing toast with the same id is replaced: the newest place, a fresh timer. |
| Remove from code | `toast.dismiss(id)`, `toast.clear()` | Removes one toast, or every toast, at once. |
| Where the toasts appear | `placement` on the `Toaster`: `'bottom'` (the default) or `'top'` | The edge for every toast, unless an open screen moves them. |
| Move the toasts while a screen is open | `useToastPlacement('top')` in the screen's component | While the component is mounted, every toast appears at that edge; when it unmounts, where it appeared before. The screen that opened last decides. `useToastPlacement(null)` moves nothing. |
| Keep clear of a bar | `offset` on the `Toaster`; the second argument of `useToastPlacement`, for the bar of the screen that moved the toasts | The height of a bar that stays at the toasts' edge, in px at the usual text size. Default 0. |
