# badge

A badge tags an item with a short fact, such as “gpu”, “12 episodes” or “v2.4.1”, so people can scan a list and tell items apart. The state of an object, such as “running”, “failed” or “needs attention”, is a <C slug="status" /> instead.

  <Tones />
</Example>

## when to use

- To tag an item with a short attribute that helps people scan a list and tell items apart: “gpu”, “read-only”, “4k”, “hdr”.
- For a count beside the thing it counts: “12 episodes”, “3 new”.
- For a short label that stays true for as long as the item exists: a version, such as “v2.4.1”, or a “default” marker.

## when to use something else

- **For the state of an object.** Use a <C slug="status" />. It shows each state in the colour of its meaning, with a <Term id="glyph">glyph</Term> of its own, and it announces warnings and errors in words. A badge's tone is only a colour: nothing stops a badge from saying “failed” in green.
- **For a message about the page or a task**, such as a save that worked or a list that could not load. Use a <C slug="banner" />, or a <C slug="toast" /> for something that just finished.
- **As a button, or to filter a list.** A badge is static: it can't take <Term id="focus">focus</Term> or be pressed. Use a <C slug="button" />, or <C slug="filter-tabs" /> to filter a list.
- **For a count on a tile.** A <C slug="tile" /> on the <Term id="home">home page</Term> has a badge of its own.

## anatomy

1. **Frame.** A small square frame with a thin border, a little taller than its text. A neutral badge sits on a slightly lighter panel. A badge with a tone sits on a faint <Term id="tint">tint</Term> of its colour, and its border mixes that colour with the usual border colour.
2. **Dot** (optional). A small square in the label's colour, just before the text. It is hidden from <Term id="assistive-technology">assistive technology</Term>.
3. **Label.** One or two words in small <Term id="monospace">monospace</Term>, slightly spaced out and in lowercase. It never wraps.

## variants and states

There are four tones. Neutral is the usual one, and the right choice for most tags. A tone is only a colour, so use one where it helps people find a label, and keep what it means the same everywhere.

| tone | looks | use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral | Grey text on a slightly lighter panel | Tags, counts and versions: the usual choice |
| Blue | Blue text on a faint blue tint | Information worth spotting: “new”, “default” |
| Green | Green text on a faint green tint | A positive attribute: “verified”, “included” |
| Amber | Amber text on a faint amber tint | An attribute that asks for care: “read-only”, “deprecated” |

There is no red tone. An error is a state: a <C slug="status" /> for one object, a <C slug="banner" /> for the page.

A badge can have a dot: a small square in the label's colour, before the text. The dot adds no meaning of its own, because it only repeats the tone, and it makes a badge look like a status light. Use it as a colour key, where the same colour appears nearby, as in a legend. If you reach for it to show a state, you want a status.

## behaviour

- A badge is static: it has no focus, no keys and no action of its own. <Kbd>tab</Kbd> passes over it.
- It never wraps, and it has no maximum width: a long label runs out of its container rather than breaking onto a second line.

The full keyboard map is on the badge's [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-data-display-badge--docs).

## accessibility

- A <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> reads a badge's text along with the words around it.
- The tone is never announced, and neither is the dot. The words carry the meaning: “read-only” is read out, the amber is not.
- The lowercase look is only a style, so screen readers read the text as you wrote it.
- A bare number is read without its subject: just “12”. Keep a count right next to what it counts, or say what it counts: “12 files”.
- The text of every tone has enough <Term id="contrast">contrast</Term> on its own tint to be read easily. The frame is only decoration.

## content

- One or two words, in lowercase, with no full stop: “gpu”, “read-only”, “3 new”.
- Say what the item is, not what it is doing right now. “running” is a state; “gpu” is a tag.
- Use digits for counts: “12”, not “twelve”.
- Don't repeat the column or field name in every badge: “gpu”, not “label: gpu”.

## do and don't

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="Badges as tags: they say what the item is. Neutral, unless a colour helps.">
    <Badge>gpu</Badge>
    <Badge>batch</Badge>
    <Badge>stateful</Badge>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="A state in a badge. The tone is only a colour, so nothing stops it from saying “failed” in green. Use a status.">
    <Badge tone="green">failed</Badge>
    <Badge tone="amber">running</Badge>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="Neutral by default, and one tone where it helps people find a label.">
    <Badge>v2.4.1</Badge>
    <Badge>12 files</Badge>
    <Badge tone="blue">new</Badge>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="A colour on every tag means nothing, and people look for a pattern that isn't there.">
    <Badge tone="blue">gpu</Badge>
    <Badge tone="green">batch</Badge>
    <Badge tone="amber">http</Badge>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <Do caption="A word or two. The explanation goes in the text beside it.">
    <Badge tone="amber">read-only</Badge>
  </Do>
  <Dont caption="A sentence in a badge doesn't wrap: it runs out of its container.">
    <Badge tone="amber">read-only until the nightly backup has finished</Badge>
  </Dont>
</DoDont>

## specs

| part | spec |
| --- | --- |
| Frame | Inline, 20px tall, padding 0 8px, a 1px border, `border-radius: 0`. Neutral: a `--surface-2` fill and a `--border-2` frame. A tone: a 12% mix of its colour over `--bg-2`, framed in a 45% mix of its colour and `--border-2`. |
| Dot | `dot`: a 6px square in the label's colour, 8px before the text, hidden from assistive technology (`aria-hidden`). |
| Label | JetBrains Mono 11px, line height 1, letter-spacing 0.02em, lowercased with `text-transform`. It never wraps (`white-space: nowrap`) and has no maximum width. |

| tone | in code | text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral | `tone="neutral"` (the default) | `--fg-muted` |
| Blue | `tone="blue"` | `--cloud-blue` |
| Green | `tone="green"` | `--signal-green` |
| Amber | `tone="amber"` | `--signal-amber` |

- **Contrast:** every tone's text meets 4.5:1 on its own tint; `green` is the closest, at about 4.9:1. The frame is decorative.
- **Element:** a plain `span` with no role. It takes no focus and has no keys, so `tab` skips it.
