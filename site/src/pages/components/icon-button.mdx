# icon button

An icon button is a <C slug="button" /> that shows only an icon, in the same kinds and sizes. Its label is required: it isn't drawn, but it becomes the button's <Term id="accessible-name">accessible name</Term>.

</Example>

## when to use

- In tight places where a text label doesn't fit: a toolbar, the header of a card or a panel, a table row. In a table row, keep it to one or two; more go into an <C slug="action-menu" />.
- For actions people recognise from the icon alone: copy, refresh, close, delete.
- Next to a field or a title, for a small action on it, such as copying a value or opening a log.

## when to use something else

- **When there is room for a label, or for the main action of the screen.** Use a <C slug="button" /> with a label, and the icon before it if it helps. A word is read faster than an icon is worked out.
- **For a setting that is on or off.** Use a <C slug="switch" />. The icon button has no pressed look. A state of what is on the screen, such as a saved title, is the planned icon button that is [on and off](#on-and-off).
- **For more than three or four actions side by side.** Put them in a <C slug="toolbar">toolbar</C>, and let the rest go into an action menu.
- **For several actions on every row of a table.** Give each row one action menu, and put the actions for several rows in a selection toolbar. [Selection and bulk actions](/design-system/patterns/selection-and-bulk-actions/) shows how.
- **To go somewhere.** Use a link. A button that changes the page breaks “open in new tab” and the browser's back button.

## anatomy

1. **Frame.** A square with a thin border, in one of three sizes. The icon sits in its centre.
2. **Icon.** The picture of the action, sized to match the button. While the button is busy, a <C slug="spinner" /> takes its place.
3. **Label.** Not drawn. It is the button's name, and the browser shows it in a small box of its own while the pointer rests on the button.

## variants and states

An icon button comes in the same four kinds as a button. Only the emphasized one is filled with colour, because it marks the next step.

| kind | looks | use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emphasized | Filled with blue, with a dark icon; a lighter blue under the pointer | The one <Term id="emphasized-action">emphasized action</Term> of a toolbar or a panel |
| Standard | A dark fill and a thin border, which turns blue under the pointer | An icon action that stands on its own |
| Quiet | No fill, no border and a slightly dimmer icon, until the pointer rests on it | Rows of actions, in a toolbar or a table row |
| Danger | An amber outline and icon, which fill with amber under the pointer | An action that deletes something or can't be undone |

Icon buttons come in three sizes, the same three as every other control. The small one suits table rows and dense toolbars, the medium one is the usual choice, and the large one sits beside a large input or button. Match the height of the controls next to it.

## on and off

Some icon buttons hold a state that people turn on and off where they are: a title saved to a list, a title they like, subtitles shown in a player. A press turns such a button on, and it stays on, and looks on, until the next press. The on look is planned: the package doesn't draw it yet, and the drawings show the idea.

### when it fits

Three controls go back and forth between two states, and each fits its own case:

| what it is | use | its label | for example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A setting that lasts, in a list or a panel of settings | A <C slug="switch" /> | The setting's name, the same in both states | “Email alerts” |
| A state of the thing on the screen, changed where people act, where a switch and its words don't fit | An icon button that is on or off | One action for both states, verb first | “save to watchlist”, “like”, “show subtitles” |
| Two states that need two verbs | An icon button whose label and icon change, with no on look | What the next press does | “play”, then “pause” |

<figure class="ds-figure">
  <WhichOneFits />
  <figcaption>A setting is a switch. A state of the thing on the screen is an icon button that is on or off. Two states that need two verbs are one button whose label and icon change with each press.</figcaption>
</figure>

- **The label stays an action.** It is the action that turns the button on, and it doesn't change when the button does, as <Term id="aria">ARIA</Term>'s guidance for toggle buttons asks. A <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> adds the state in its own words, such as “pressed”. A label that changed as well would say the state twice, or contradict it: “remove from watchlist”, pressed, says the title is removed while it is saved.
- **When one verb can't serve both states,** as with play and pause, the button isn't on or off. It says what its next press does, and its icon changes with its label.
- **Where there is room for words, a setting stays a switch.** An icon button that is on or off is for the places a switch and its label don't fit: a toolbar, a player's controls, the actions of a title.

### the on look

On adds two things to the icon button:

1. **Bar.** A blue bar along the bottom edge, inside the frame: the same mark as the line under the current tab. It is the shape that says on, so the state never rests on colour alone.
2. **Tint.** The faint blue of a selected row, behind the icon. On its own it is too faint to see; it only backs the bar.

- **The icon stays as it is**, a line drawing like every icon ([iconography](/design-system/styles/iconography/)), in the brightest grey.
- **Only the standard and the quiet kinds can be on.** The emphasized kind is the next step, and the danger kind deletes something: neither holds a state.
- **Under the pointer,** an icon button that is on keeps its bar, over the usual hover fill. Keyboard <Term id="focus">focus</Term> is the outline every control shows.
- **Not a blue fill.** A filled blue icon button is the one <Term id="emphasized-action">emphasized action</Term> of a view, so toggles filled blue would read as more next steps.
- **On a TV,** the bar stays while the button has focus, and the ring stays the sign of focus alone ([TV focus](/design-system/patterns/tv-focus/#one-meaning-for-the-ring)).

### behaviour and accessibility

- A press turns it on or off: a click or a tap, or <Kbd>enter</Kbd> or <Kbd>space</Kbd> on the keyboard. The change applies at once, as a switch's does, and focus stays on the button.
- If applying the change fails, the button goes back, and the page says why.
- When the effect shows somewhere else, confirm it with a <C slug="toast" />: “Saved to watchlist”.
- It is a toggle button: it tells <Term id="assistive-technology">assistive technology</Term> whether it is pressed, and a screen reader announces both states.
- The bar contrasts 6.85 to 1 with the tint, above the 3 to 1 that <Term id="wcag">WCAG</Term> 1.4.11 asks of a part that shows a state. The tint is 1.05 to 1 against the surface, which is why it never shows the state on its own.

Today: nothing draws the on look. The icon button passes `aria-pressed` on to its button, so a screen reader would hear a state that people who see the screen can't. Until the look is built, use a switch, or a button whose label and icon change.

| state | in code (planned) | looks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Off | `aria-pressed="false"` | The kind as it is, standard or quiet |
| On | `aria-pressed="true"` | A `--surface-selected` fill, a 2px `--cloud-blue` rule inside the bottom edge, drawn as an inset shadow so the size stays the same, and the icon in `--fg` |
| On, under the pointer | `aria-pressed="true"` and hover | A `--surface-2` fill and the rule kept; the standard kind's border turns `--cloud-blue`, as on any hover |

## behaviour

- An icon button acts on the current screen when it is pressed: with a click or a tap, or with <Kbd>enter</Kbd> or <Kbd>space</Kbd> on the keyboard.
- A busy or disabled icon button ignores presses.
- While the mouse pointer rests on it, the browser shows the label in a small box of its own, after a short delay. It never shows on keyboard focus or on touch.

The full keyboard map is on the icon button's [React page](/design-system/react/?path=/docs/components-actions-icon-button--docs).

## accessibility

- The label is required, and it is the button's whole name. Write it exactly as it should be read.
- The browser shows the same words as its own tooltip. <Term id="assistive-technology">Assistive technology</Term> may read them a second time, as a description. That tooltip has none of our styling, and it appears only on mouse <Term id="hover">hover</Term>.
- People who see the screen but use a keyboard or a touch screen never see the label. Use icons whose meaning is not in doubt, or a <C slug="button" /> with a visible label.
- The icon is decoration, hidden from assistive technology: the label names the action.
- A <Term id="screen-reader">screen reader</Term> may not announce that the button is busy. When the outcome matters, announce it elsewhere, in a <C slug="toast" /> for instance.
- Keyboard <Term id="focus">focus</Term> shows as the same outline that every control uses. [Interaction](/design-system/foundations/interaction/#focus) explains it.

Today:

- A busy icon button is disabled, and a focused button that becomes disabled can lose focus. People who pressed it with a keyboard or a screen reader can be moved off it while it loads.
- There is no pressed state. For on and off, use a <C slug="switch" />.
- Don't wrap an icon button in a <C slug="tooltip" />. The tooltip's wrapper is a <Term id="tab-stop">tab stop</Term> of its own, so the pair takes two tab stops. The description lands on the wrapper instead of the button, and the browser's own tooltip still appears.

## content

- The label is an action, verb first, in lowercase: “restart worker”, “copy link”, “delete volume”. Name the action, not the icon, so not “arrow” or “trash”.
- When the button repeats on every row or card, name the object too: “delete api-7f9c”, not “delete”. Otherwise a screen reader's list of buttons is a column of identical names.
- One icon, one meaning, everywhere. An icon that stands for two actions can no longer be read as either.

## specs

| kind | in code | at rest | under the pointer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emphasized | `variant="primary"` | `--cloud-blue` fill and border, `--bg` icon | A lighter blue (`#6cb2ff`) fill and border |
| Standard | `variant="default"` (the default) | `--surface` fill, `--border-2` border, `--fg` icon | `--surface-2` fill and a `--cloud-blue` border |
| Quiet | `variant="ghost"` | No fill or border, `--fg-2` icon | `--surface-2` fill and a `--fg` icon |
| Danger | `variant="danger"` | `--signal-amber` border and icon, no fill | `--signal-amber` fill and a `--bg` icon |

| size | in code | square | icon | spinner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | `size="sm"` | 28px | 12px | 12px |
| Medium | `size="md"` (the default) | 34px | 16px | 14px |
| Large | `size="lg"` | 42px | 20px | 14px |

- **Frame:** square (`border-radius: 0`), a 1px border, the icon centred.
- **Icon:** the child you pass, such as `<Icon icon={RotateCw} />`. The button doesn't size it: `Icon` is 16px by default, which suits `md`, and the sizes example passes `size={12}` at `sm` and `size={20}` at `lg` (see [iconography](/design-system/styles/iconography/)). It is hidden from assistive tech.
- **Label:** `label`, required. It becomes `aria-label`, and the native `title` that the browser shows as its own tooltip.
- **Busy:** `loading`. A spinner in place of the icon, the size unchanged, `aria-busy="true"`. The button is disabled while busy, so it ignores presses and leaves the tab order.
- **Disabled:** `disabled`. 45% opacity, a not-allowed cursor, out of the tab order.
- **Focus:** the 2px `--focus-ring` outline, 1px outside the frame, on keyboard focus only.
- **Motion:** background, border and icon colours change in 120ms `ease`.
- **Pressed:** no pressed look today.
