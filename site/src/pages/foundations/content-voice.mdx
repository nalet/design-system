# content & voice

Two voices, one per typeface; short, and true. The words on a screen say what is so and what the person can do — in their language, not the system's.

## the voice

The system speaks with two voices, and the typeface tells them apart.

- **Monospace is the system's voice, and it is lowercase.** Titles and headings; buttons, tabs and navigation; status chips and badges, table and column headers; menu items, toolbar and filter labels; numbers and codes. It is short and scannable, and the lowercase is part of the identity, in every language. Names and codes inside it keep their own case: a series called *The Quiet Harbour* stays *The Quiet Harbour* in a group label, an episode stays `S04E07`, an ID stays as it is. The components set `text-transform: lowercase` on this chrome, and only on it.
- **The body face is the reader's language, and it follows that language's own rules.** Everything that explains: descriptions and subtitles; messages in message strips, toasts and dialog bodies; empty-state descriptions; field labels, hints and errors; checkbox and switch labels; and the prose of these guidelines. It is written in sentence case: a capital at the start of each sentence, for names, for "I", for acronyms (TMDB, HEVC, GB, WCAG, CSS, URL), and for German nouns. Nothing lowercases it.
- **Why two.** The brand lives in the monospace layer, where text is short and set apart. Readability and correct spelling in every language live in the layer people actually read.
- **Short.** A label is two or three words; a message one or two sentences. Cut adverbs, cut "please", cut "successfully".
- **Plain.** No emoji, no exclamation marks, no marketing words, no jokes in error messages.
- **Direct.** Address the reader ("pick a release"), name the actor when it matters ("the catalog holds them without an identity"), and use the active voice.

## write what is true, then what to do

| part | says | example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A status | The state in the object's words | `on its way`, `needs attention`, `connection firewalled` |
| Its detail | Why, or what happens next | `nothing found · again in 6h`, `the release was removed by its source` |
| A message | What is true and what it means | `105 titles can't be checked: the catalog holds them without an identity.` |
| An action | A verb and its object | `ask the catalog again`, `grab 8 selected`, `clear filters` |
| An empty state | What is true, why, and the way forward | `no matches` · `Nothing in this list fits the search and filters.` · `clear filters` |

## primary text stays human

IDs, codes, raw errors and HTTP statuses help an operator fix things — so they are kept, but out of the sentence. A <C slug="message-strip" /> puts them under its **details** disclosure; a table puts them behind a per-row `<details>`; a status cell keeps a raw search error on hover. The primary text is what a person can act on without reading the system's internals.

## buttons start with a verb

- `save changes`, `grab 8 selected`, `ask the catalog again`, `pick a release`.
- Name the object when the screen holds more than one kind; put the count in a bulk action.
- Never `ok`, `submit`, `yes`, `click here`, `learn more`.
- The confirming button of a dialog repeats the title's verb (`delete volume` → `delete volume`); the way out is `cancel`.

## numbers and dates

- Counts use thousands separators: `2,500`, not `2500` (`formatCount`).
- A date is said both ways: absolute and relative — `12 oct 2026 · 5d ago` (<C slug="table-cells">date cell</C>).
- `n of m` reads shown-of-total: `100 of 2,500`.
- Plurals agree: `1 title`, `105 titles`; `8 episodes queued`, `1 movie queued`.

## words that mean one thing

| use | for | not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `missing` | Wanted and not held | "absent", "lacking" |
| `on its way` | Queued, grabbed, downloading, waiting to be listed | "in progress" for things that are not |
| `needs attention` | Failed or blocked, a person must act | "error" on its own, "problem" |
| `can't be checked` | The system cannot know | "unknown" with no reason |
| `held` | In the library | "done" |

See [status vocabulary](/design-system/patterns/status-vocabulary/) for how a domain writes its own.
